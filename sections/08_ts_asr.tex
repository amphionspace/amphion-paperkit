\section{Target-Speaker Recognition}
\label{sec:tsasr}

\subsection{Setup}

We evaluate target-speaker recognition (TS-ASR) on three test sets:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Internal hot+TS test set (\texttt{ts\_hw\_test}).}
        Generated by the same
        \path{local/prepare_tsasr_data.py} pipeline as the
        training cuts (Section~\ref{subsec:tsasr-synth}) but with the
        held-out config \path{configs/target_speaker/synth_test.json}:
        $\texttt{seed}=124$ guarantees zero overlap with training,
        $\texttt{max\_total\_samples}=10{,}000$ caps the size,
        $\texttt{samples\_per\_speaker}=40$ widens speaker coverage,
        and \texttt{enable\_rir} is turned off so reverberation is no
        longer a confound. Source pool covers the test splits of all
        ten Mandarin and four English ASR corpora used in synthesis.
        Five percent of cuts are anti-hallucination negatives.
  \item \textbf{LibriMix~2 (Libri2Mix).} The standard public
        two-speaker mixture benchmark derived from
        LibriSpeech~\cite{cosentino2020librimix}; we use the
        \texttt{test / 16k / min / mix\_both} split (6{,}000 cuts,
        absolute audio paths embedded in the \texttt{cuts.jsonl.gz}).
  \item \textbf{LibriMix~3 (Libri3Mix).} Same release, three-speaker
        mixtures (9{,}000 cuts).
\end{itemize}

The prompt template is the Swift-style \texttt{ts\_asr} entry of
Table~\ref{tab:tasks}: a 3--5\,s enrollment audio segment is supplied
as the first OpenAI-style \verb|{"type": "audio"}| content item, the
mixed audio as the second, and the instruction
``\texttt{Given the speaker's voice:\{enroll\}\textbackslash n
Transcribe what this speaker says in the following audio.\{speech\}}''
ties them together. We report MER on the internal set (mixed
Mandarin--English vocabulary) and WER on LibriMix; for the negative
sub-slice of \texttt{ts\_hw\_test} we additionally compute
\texttt{compute\_silence\_metrics}, the recall of empty hypotheses,
to verify that anti-hallucination training is taking effect.

\subsection{Analysis}

\paragraph{Strong on the in-domain set.}
$14.29$\% MER on the internal hot+TS set indicates that when the model
sees mixtures whose acoustic conditions and reference-utterance pipeline
match the training distribution, the prompt-based target-speaker path
works as intended.

\paragraph{Substantial gap on LibriMix.}
LibriMix numbers are much weaker -- $65.83$\% WER on Libri2Mix and
$87.06$\% on Libri3Mix. The training synthesiser already includes
LibriSpeech speakers (Table~\ref{tab:tsasr_sources}) so a pure
speaker-coverage hypothesis is unlikely. The remaining suspects are
distributional rather than identity-based:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item LibriMix uses \texttt{min} / \texttt{mix\_both} mixtures with
        a near-uniform overlap fraction, whereas our synthesiser
        truncates each interferer to a target-conditional overlap
        $\rho \!\sim\! \mathcal{N}(0.35, 0.2^2)$ clipped to
        $[0.1, 1.0]$ (Section~\ref{subsec:tsasr-synth}); the upper
        tail of LibriMix overlaps is therefore under-represented at
        training time.
  \item LibriMix samples interferers with no SNR control on top of the
        WHAM! noise, whereas our pipeline draws SNR from
        $\mathcal{N}(5, 7^2)$\,dB clipped to $[-5, 20]$.
  \item The released checkpoint enables RIR augmentation by default
        (\texttt{apply\_prob}=$0.5$); the LibriMix audio is
        anechoic, so the model encounters slightly more reverberation
        at training time than at evaluation.
\end{enumerate}
We treat this gap as a known issue and call it out explicitly in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{Hotwords + target speaker (combined).}
The released checkpoint supports both hotword and target-speaker
prompting simultaneously: in the Swift template, a \verb|Hotwords:|
line is appended after the speaker instruction (Table~\ref{tab:tasks},
\texttt{ts\_asr} row), so the model jointly conditions on the
enrollment audio and the in-prompt biasing list. A dedicated combined
benchmark (TS-ASR with $k$ hotwords supplied) is in preparation; we
report it in a future revision once the test-set construction
finalises.
