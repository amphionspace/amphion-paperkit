\section{Target-Speaker Recognition}
\label{sec:tsasr}

\subsection{Setup}

We evaluate target-speaker recognition (TS-ASR) on three test sets:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Internal hot+TS test set.} Generated by the same
        offline TS-ASR synthesiser as the training cuts
        (Section~\ref{subsec:tsasr-synth}) but with a held-out test
        configuration: a different random seed guarantees zero overlap
        with training, the total budget is capped at $10{,}000$ cuts,
        the per-speaker quota is widened to $40$ to broaden speaker
        coverage, and RIR augmentation is turned off so that
        reverberation is no longer a confound. The source pool covers
        the test splits of all ten Mandarin and four English ASR
        corpora used in training-time synthesis. Five percent of cuts
        are anti-hallucination negatives.
  \item \textbf{LibriMix~2 (Libri2Mix).} The standard public
        two-speaker mixture benchmark derived from LibriSpeech
        \cite{cosentino2020librimix}; we use the
        \texttt{test/16k/min/mix\_both} split ($6{,}000$ cuts).
  \item \textbf{LibriMix~3 (Libri3Mix).} Same release, three-speaker
        mixtures ($9{,}000$ cuts).
\end{itemize}

The prompt template is the Swift-style TS-ASR entry of
Table~\ref{tab:tasks}: a 3--5\,s enrollment audio segment is supplied
as the first audio content item, the mixed audio as the second, and
the instruction ``\textit{Given the speaker's voice} \{\textit{enroll}\}
\textit{transcribe what this speaker says in the following audio.}''
ties them together. We report MER on the internal set (mixed
Mandarin--English vocabulary) and WER on LibriMix; for the negative
sub-slice we additionally report silence recall (the empty-hypothesis
rate) to verify that anti-hallucination training is taking effect.

\subsection{Analysis}

\paragraph{Strong on the in-domain set.}
$14.29$\% MER on the internal hot+TS set indicates that when the model
sees mixtures whose acoustic conditions and reference-utterance pipeline
match the training distribution, the prompt-based target-speaker path
works as intended.

\paragraph{Substantial gap on LibriMix.}
LibriMix numbers are much weaker -- $65.83$\% WER on Libri2Mix and
$87.06$\% on Libri3Mix. The training synthesiser already includes
LibriSpeech speakers (Table~\ref{tab:tsasr_sources}) so a pure
speaker-coverage hypothesis is unlikely. The remaining suspects are
distributional rather than identity-based:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item LibriMix uses min / mix-both mixtures with a near-uniform
        overlap fraction, whereas our synthesiser truncates each
        interferer to a target-conditional overlap
        $\rho\!\sim\!\mathcal{N}(0.35, 0.2^2)$ clipped to $[0.1, 1.0]$
        (Section~\ref{subsec:tsasr-synth}); the upper tail of LibriMix
        overlaps is therefore under-represented at training time.
  \item LibriMix samples interferers with no SNR control on top of
        the WHAM! noise, whereas our pipeline draws SNR from
        $\mathcal{N}(5, 7^2)$\,dB clipped to $[-5, 20]$.
  \item The released checkpoint applies RIR augmentation with
        probability $0.5$ during training, whereas LibriMix audio is
        anechoic; the model therefore encounters slightly more
        reverberation at training time than at evaluation.
\end{enumerate}
We treat this gap as a known issue and call it out explicitly in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{Hotwords + target speaker (combined).}
The released checkpoint supports both hotword and target-speaker
prompting simultaneously: in the Swift template
(Table~\ref{tab:tasks}, TS-ASR row), a hotword line is appended after
the speaker instruction, so the model jointly conditions on the
enrollment audio and the in-prompt biasing list. A dedicated combined
benchmark (TS-ASR with $k$ hotwords supplied) is in preparation and
will appear in a future revision.

\paragraph{Anti-hallucination.}
On the negative sub-slice of the internal hot+TS test set, the model
achieves a silence recall above $90$\,\%
