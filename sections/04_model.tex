\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Overview}

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the overall design. AmphionASR is a
three-block model: a speech encoder produces frame-level
representations; a small adapter downsamples and projects them into the
LLM's token space; the LLM decoder produces the transcript
auto-regressively, conditioned on a structured prompt that may carry a
hotword list and a target-speaker reference.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering

    backbone (Qwen3-4B-Base) is unlocked progressively across the three
    training stages described in Section~\ref{sec:training}. Hotwords
    enter the prompt as a plain text list; a target-speaker reference
    enters as either a reference utterance or a speaker identifier.}

\subsection{Audio Encoder}

We use the encoder of Whisper-large-v3 \citep{radford2023whisper}: 32
Transformer layers with hidden size 1280, producing a 50\,Hz frame-level
representation from a log-Mel spectrogram. The encoder is frozen during
all training stages; this preserves Whisper's robustness on noisy and
out-of-domain audio while letting the adapter and LLM specialise to the
text decoder we wire up.

\subsection{Adapter}

The adapter is a stride-4 convolutional stack that lowers the encoder
frame rate from 50\,Hz to 12.5\,Hz, followed by a two-layer MLP that
projects to the LLM's hidden size of 2560. Total adapter size is
approximately 25\,M parameters.

The adapter is the only fully trainable module in Stage~1 (audio--text
alignment, see Section~\ref{sec:training}).

\subsection{LLM Decoder}

The decoder is Qwen3-4B-Base \citep{qwen2025qwen3}: 36 Transformer
layers, hidden size 2560, FFN intermediate size 9728, grouped-query
attention with 32 query heads and 8 key/value heads
(head dim 128), 32K context length, and a 151{,}936-token tokenizer
shared with the rest of the Qwen3 family. We retain SwiGLU activations,
RMSNorm pre-normalization, RoPE positional encoding and QK-norm as
inherited from the Qwen3-Base configuration.

\subsection{Hotword Conditioning}
\label{subsec:hotword-mech}

Hotwords are passed to the model as a plain text list inside the
prompt, before the audio token segment. No architectural change is
needed. Concretely, the prompt template includes a
\verb|<|hotwords\verb|>| field whose payload is up to twenty
comma-separated terms; during training the model sees a mixture of
empty, true-positive (terms that appear in the transcript) and
distractor lists so that it learns to bias toward terms only when they
are acoustically supported.

\subsection{Target-Speaker Conditioning}
\label{subsec:tsasr-mech}

Target-speaker recognition is also handled through the prompt: the
\verb|<|target\verb|>| field can carry either a short reference
utterance (encoded by the same audio encoder and projected by the same
adapter, then concatenated as additional audio tokens before the main
audio segment) or a speaker identifier from a known enrollment set.

\subsection{Inference}

At inference time we deploy the model with vLLM \citep{kwon2023vllm}
behind a batched HTTP endpoint
(\texttt{src/integrations/scripts/deploy/serve\_vllm.sh} in the
released code). We use greedy decoding by default, with a
beam search of width 4 reserved for the headline numbers reported in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.
