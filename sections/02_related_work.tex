\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{End-to-end ASR.}
% TODO: CTC / RNN-T / AED briefly. Conformer \citep{gulati2020conformer}.
End-to-end ASR replaces traditional pipelines with a single neural model
\citep{graves2006ctc, graves2012rnnt, chan2016las, gulati2020conformer}.

\paragraph{Self-supervised speech representations.}
% TODO: wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT, WavLM, BEATs.
Self-supervised pre-training on unlabeled audio
\citep{baevski2020wav2vec2, hsu2021hubert} provides strong encoders that
transfer well to downstream ASR with limited supervision.

\paragraph{Large-scale weakly-supervised ASR.}
% TODO: Whisper, USM, Canary, OWSM.
Whisper \citep{radford2023whisper} and follow-up systems demonstrated the
benefit of scaling weakly-supervised data, achieving robust performance
across domains and languages.

\paragraph{LLM-based and audio-LLM ASR.}
% TODO: SLAM-ASR, Qwen-Audio, SALMONN, Step-Audio, Kimi-Audio, Seed-ASR.
Recent work couples speech encoders with pre-trained LLMs as the decoder,
yielding systems that share a single backbone for transcription and
downstream language tasks \citep{chu2023qwenaudio, tang2024salmonn}.
Our work follows this line and contributes a careful study of data mixing,
adapter design, and staged training.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
Compared with prior audio-LLMs that focus on general audio understanding,
\textbf{LLM-ASR} is optimized specifically for transcription quality and
contextual ASR while preserving the LLM's instruction-following abilities.
