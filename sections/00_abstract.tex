\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{AmphionASR}, a 4.3\,B parameter speech recognizer that
couples a Whisper-large-v3 audio encoder with the Qwen3-4B-Base large
language model through a lightweight convolution-plus-MLP adapter.
Beyond producing competitive Mandarin and English transcripts, AmphionASR
is designed for two real-world capabilities that are awkward for purely
end-to-end ASR systems: \emph{hotword conditioning} -- the model
accepts a small list of likely terms in its prompt and reliably biases
its output toward them -- and \emph{target-speaker recognition} -- given
a short reference utterance the model transcribes only the matching
speaker in mixed audio.
On the standard public benchmarks our model reaches
% TODO: insert headline numbers from main results once baseline rows are filled in
$1.8$\% CER on AISHELL-1, $7.17$\% WER on LibriSpeech \texttt{test-clean}
and $14.99$\% MER on the TALCS Mandarin--English code-switching test set.
With as few as five in-prompt hotwords, the CommonVoice CER drops from
$6.57$\% to $2.83$\% (Mandarin) and the WER from $9.65$\% to $4.89$\%
(English). On our internal hot-word-and-target-speaker test set we obtain
$14.29$\% MER. We discuss current gaps -- in particular weak performance
on the LibriMix open multi-talker benchmarks and an as-yet-unsupported
explicit noise-robustness path -- and outline the next training
iteration. We will release model weights and inference code at
\url{https://github.com/amphionspace/AmphionASR} % TODO: verify the public URL
.
\end{abstract}
