\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{AmphionASR}, an open SpeechLLM for Mandarin and
English speech recognition. The model couples a 314\,M-parameter
Whisper-style audio tower (\textbf{Qwen3-AuT}; 24-layer Transformer at
$d{=}1024$) to the \textbf{Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507} large language model
through a 9.2\,M-parameter Linear--SwooshR--Linear projector and four
learnable modality-boundary prompt vectors. Total parameter count is
approximately $4.74$\,B ($4.3$\,B distinct after \texttt{lm\_head}
tying). Beyond producing competitive Mandarin and English transcripts,
AmphionASR is designed for two real-world capabilities that are awkward
for purely end-to-end ASR systems: \emph{hotword conditioning} -- the
model accepts a small list of likely terms in its prompt and reliably
biases output toward them -- and \emph{target-speaker recognition} --
given a short reference utterance the model transcribes only the
matching speaker in mixed audio.
On the standard public benchmarks our v3 checkpoint reaches
$1.8$\% CER on AISHELL-1, $7.17$\% WER on LibriSpeech \texttt{test-clean}
and $14.99$\% MER on the TALCS Mandarin--English code-switching test
set. With as few as five in-prompt hotwords, the CommonVoice CER drops
from $6.57$\% to $2.83$\% (Mandarin) and the WER from $9.65$\% to
$4.89$\% (English). On our internal hotword + target-speaker test set
we obtain $14.29$\% MER. We discuss current gaps -- in particular weak
performance on the LibriMix open multi-talker benchmarks and an
as-yet-unsupported explicit noise-robustness path -- and outline the
next training iteration. We will release model weights and inference
code at \url{https://github.com/amphionspace/AmphionASR}

\end{abstract}
