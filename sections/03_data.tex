\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

All training and evaluation data are organised as
Lhotse~\cite{zelasko2021lhotse} \texttt{recordings}, \texttt{supervisions}
and \texttt{cuts}. The full multi-task training mixture
(\texttt{configs/target\_speaker/train.yaml}) jointly draws from seven
task families:
plain Mandarin / English / multilingual ASR,
hotword-conditioned ASR,
target-speaker ASR (TS-ASR),
speech emotion recognition (SER),
speech emotion captioning (SEC) and
environmental sound captioning (ESC). Tables~\ref{tab:data_asr} through
\ref{tab:data_aux} list the underlying sources; Sections~\ref{subsec:tsasr-synth}
and \ref{subsec:esc-mix} describe the two on-disk / on-the-fly
synthesis pipelines.

\paragraph{Language tag canonicalisation.}
Source manifests use a mixture of ISO-639-1, ISO-639-3 and locale tags
(\texttt{zh}, \texttt{zh-cn}, \texttt{en-us}, \texttt{eng}). The
\texttt{\_LANGUAGE\_NORMALIZE\_MAP} in
\texttt{src/integrations/ms\_swift/data/convert.py} normalises every
\texttt{supervision.language} to a Title-Case English name (e.g.\
\texttt{zh}\,$\to$\,Chinese, \texttt{en-us}\,$\to$\,English) across
zh / en / ja / ko / fr / de / es / ru / pt / it / nl / tr / ar / hi /
vi / th / id / ms; unknown values are preserved verbatim and warned
once per worker.

\subsection{Hotword-Conditioned ASR Sources}
\label{subsec:hotword-sources}

Three of the public ASR datasets carry an extra
\texttt{custom.hotwords} field on each \texttt{supervision} that
contains the ground-truth rare entities for that utterance
(extracted offline by a domain-name miner; see
Table~\ref{tab:data_hotwords}). At training time we do not re-mix the
audio; the same waveform is paired with the hotword-bearing
supervisions to teach the model the prompt-conditioned biasing
behaviour. Two CommonVoice splits with the same annotation are reserved
for evaluation.

\subsection{Augmentation Resources}
\label{subsec:aug-resources}

Two pools of side data are used by the TS-ASR synthesis pipeline
(Section~\ref{subsec:tsasr-synth}): room impulse responses (RIRs) and
additive noise. RIR convolutions are truncated to $1.5$\,s (early
reflections dominate intelligibility) and applied via an FFT-based
overlap-add. Noise is mixed at a configured signal-to-noise ratio
(SNR) and the result is peak-normalised to avoid clipping.

\subsection{Subsampling and Mixing}
\label{subsec:mixing}

We use Lhotse's mux to draw mini-batches uniformly across all
data sources after the per-source caps and repeat factors of
Tables~\ref{tab:data_asr}--\ref{tab:data_aux} have been applied. The
two knobs implement different objectives:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{train\_dataset\_samples} caps over-large corpora
        (WenetSpeech and GigaSpeech at 1\,M utterances; Emilia,
        WenetSpeech4TTS, MLS, AISHELL-2, CommonVoice EN, KeSpeech at
        500\,k) so that no single source dominates the mux.
  \item \texttt{train\_dataset\_reps} up-samples small corpora to a
        comparable scale: hotword splits at $r{=}2$--$3$, every SER /
        SEC source at $r{=}5$, and AudioSet ESC at $r{=}10$ (its raw
        $\sim$218\,k cuts become $\sim$2.2\,M after repetition,
        matching the major ASR streams).
\end{itemize}

The synthesised TS-ASR cut set
(Section~\ref{subsec:tsasr-synth}) is added with $r{=}1$ because the
underlying $\sim$3\,M positive cuts already match the major ASR
streams in size.

\subsection{TS-ASR Synthesis}
\label{subsec:tsasr-synth}

The target-speaker training data is produced offline by
\texttt{local/prepare\_tsasr\_data.py}. Given 16 single-speaker source
manifests (10 Mandarin + 6 English, listed in Table~\ref{tab:tsasr_sources}),
each output sample contains a target waveform mixed with
$K$ interferers, an enrollment utterance from the same speaker but
\emph{a different recording}, and the target's transcript:

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{mixed}[n] = s_\text{tgt}[n] + \sum_{k=1}^{K}\,\alpha_k\,s_{\text{int},k}[n - o_k],
  \qquad
  \alpha_k = \frac{\rho_\text{tgt}}{\rho_{\text{int},k}}\cdot 10^{-\mathrm{SNR}_k/20},
  \label{eq:mixspeakers}
\end{equation}

where $\rho$ denotes RMS energy, $o_k$ is a uniformly sampled time
offset that places interferer $k$ inside the target, and the overlap
fraction $|\text{int}_k|/|\text{tgt}| = \mathrm{Uniform}[0.1, 1.0]$
controls how much of the target the interferer covers.
The default ``clean'' configuration
(\texttt{configs/target\_speaker/synth\_template.json}) draws
$K{\sim}\mathrm{Cat}(\{1{:}0.8, 2{:}0.2\})$,
$\mathrm{SNR}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(5,7^2)$\,dB clipped to $[-5, 20]$, and
$3$--$5$\,s enrollments. After
mixing we apply an RIR with probability $0.5$
(Table~\ref{tab:data_rir_noise}) and additive background noise
(SNR drawn from $[0, 25]$\,dB).

\paragraph{Anti-hallucination negatives.}
Five to ten percent of every training shard is replaced by
\emph{negative} samples whose ground-truth transcript is the empty
string. Two sub-types are sampled with weights $\{ \texttt{noise\_only},
\texttt{distractor\_only}\}$ (Table~\ref{tab:tsasr_negatives}); both
preserve a real enrollment but remove the target speaker from the
mixed channel, training the model to remain silent when the requested
speaker is absent.

\paragraph{Output and scale.}
The default budget is $3{,}000{,}000$ training samples (with up to
$200$ samples per speaker), written as a single
\texttt{mix\_all\_cuts\_all.jsonl.gz} cut set under a structured
output directory (\texttt{mixed\_audio/<dataset>/<spk>\_mix\_<idx>.wav},
\texttt{enrollment\_audio/<dataset>/<spk>\_enroll\_<idx>.wav},
plus \texttt{\_negative/silence} and \texttt{\_negative/distractor}
sub-trees). Section~\ref{sec:tsasr} reports evaluation results on a
held-out 10\,k-cut test set (\texttt{ts\_hw\_test}) generated by the
same pipeline with $\texttt{seed}=124$ to guarantee zero overlap with
training.

\subsection{ESC Foreground Mixing}
\label{subsec:esc-mix}

Environmental sound captioning is trained with on-the-fly mixing
(\texttt{EscForegroundMix} in \path{src/esc_mixing.py}). Each AudioSet ESC
``background'' cut is paired with an Emilia
foreground utterance (DNSMOS-filtered \cite{he2024emilia, reddy2021dnsmos})
of $0.5$--$10$\,s, and combined under one of three modes drawn with
probabilities $\{$overlap$\,0.8$, prepend$\,0.1$, append$\,0.1\}$. The
SNR is $\mathrm{Uniform}(0, 20)$\,dB by default, except when the
background's \texttt{custom.label\_only\_class} is \texttt{silence},
in which case SNR is unconstrained (the foreground simply plays over
the silence). Validation and the offline ESC test set
(\texttt{audioset\_esc\_test\_mixed\_cuts.jsonl.gz}, $\sim$2{,}166 cuts)
use a fixed mix seed so that loss curves stay comparable across
checkpoints.

\subsection{Hotword Pool Construction at Training Time}
\label{subsec:hotword-pool}

Training-time hotword lists are assembled per-sample by
\texttt{build\_hotwords\_for\_sample} in
\path{src/integrations/ms_swift/data/hotwords.py}, in three stages
configured through \path{configs/target_speaker/train.yaml}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Real terms.} Each ground-truth hotword from
        \texttt{custom.hotwords} is independently dropped with
        probability $\texttt{miss\_prob}=0.05$ to simulate imperfect
        retrieval recall.
  \item \textbf{Hard negatives.} An online retriever fetches up to
        $\texttt{online\_hard\_neg\_top\_k}=10$ confusable terms per
        sample using a weighted score $0.6\cdot\text{char-overlap}
        + 0.4\cdot\text{bigram-Jaccard}$ on a per-dataset hotword
        index, augmented for Mandarin by a \texttt{pypinyin}-based
        single-syllable swap retriever.
  \item \textbf{Random distractors.} The remaining
        $n_\text{distractor}\!\in[1, 20]$ slots
        (\texttt{max\_hotwords}=20) are filled from the global hotword
        pool, with \texttt{hard\_neg\_ratio}=1.0 of slots reserved for
        hard negatives.
\end{enumerate}

The whole \texttt{Hotwords:} prompt line is then kept with probability
$\texttt{prompt\_hotword\_prob}=0.8$ and dropped otherwise; this trains
the same model to behave gracefully when no hotword side-channel is
available.
