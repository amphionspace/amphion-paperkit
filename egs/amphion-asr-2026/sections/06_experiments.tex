\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

\paragraph{Decoding.}
Unless otherwise stated we report greedy-decoded results obtained
through the released vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm} batched inference
server.

\paragraph{Normalization.}
For English benchmarks we apply the Whisper English text normalizer
\cite{radford2023whisper} to both reference and hypothesis before
computing WER. For Mandarin we apply a stripped-down normalizer that
removes punctuation, normalises full-width / half-width characters
and maps Arabic numerals to their Mandarin equivalents before CER.
For TALCS we apply the union of the two normalizers and report MER.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare AmphionASR against Whisper-large-v3
\cite{radford2023whisper} and the open-source Qwen3-ASR-1.7B
\cite{qwen2025qwen3asr}. Both baselines will be re-run through the
normalizers in this section so the numbers in
Tables~\ref{tab:asr_en}--\ref{tab:asr_cs} are directly comparable;
until that re-run lands, the baseline columns are reported as
``?'' and the discussion below refers only to the AmphionASR
column.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
On the Mandarin grid AmphionASR reaches single-digit CER on every
test set. AISHELL-1 at $1.85$\,\% CER is the headline data point on
read speech, the AISHELL-2/3 and MagicData sets sit at
$2$--$3$\,\% CER, and the spontaneous WenetSpeech subsets and the
read-but-broad-domain FLEURS~zh now also fall under $7$\,\% CER
(WenetSpeech test-net $5.94$\,\%, test-meeting $6.99$\,\%,
FLEURS~zh $3.38$\,\%). KeSpeech at $6.45$\,\% CER is the worst
Mandarin point and corresponds to the multi-dialect mixture inside
that benchmark, which we consider the natural residual.
% TODO: cite Seed-ASR / Qwen3-ASR FLEURS numbers in this paragraph
% once we have re-run them on the same normalizer.

On the English grid the model is competitive on the read-speech
LibriSpeech sets ($1.82 / 3.78$\,\% WER on
test-clean/test-other), on FLEURS~en ($5.53$\,\% WER), on MLS
($6.46$\,\% WER) and on CommonVoice~17 ($8.14$\,\% WER), with
GigaSpeech at $9.45$\,\% WER as the worst English point and
corresponding to the more spontaneous broadcast / interview domain
inside that benchmark. On the Mandarin--English code-switching
TALCS test set we reach $7.48$\,\% MER, summarising a setting in
which both languages must be produced inside the same utterance.
