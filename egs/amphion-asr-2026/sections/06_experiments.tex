\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

\paragraph{Decoding.}
Unless otherwise stated we report greedy-decoded results obtained
through the released vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm} batched inference
server.

\paragraph{Normalization.}
For English benchmarks we apply the Whisper English text normalizer
\cite{radford2023whisper} to both reference and hypothesis before
computing WER. For Mandarin we apply a stripped-down normalizer that
removes punctuation, normalises full-width / half-width characters
and maps Arabic numerals to their Mandarin equivalents before CER.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare AmphionASR against nine recent open-source speech-LLM
and dedicated ASR systems, organised loosely by training objective:
audio-understanding / omni-modal LLMs ---
Whisper-large-v3~\cite{radford2023whisper}, Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct
\cite{kimiaudio2025}, Step-Audio~2~\cite{stepaudio22025},
MiniCPM-o~\cite{minicpm2025}, MOSS-Audio~\cite{moss2026audio} --- and dedicated ASR-oriented
LLM-based systems --- Qwen3-ASR-1.7B~\cite{qwen2025qwen3asr},
FireRed-ASR2-LLM~\cite{fireredasr2025}, and
GLM-ASR-nano~\cite{glmasr2025}. The baseline columns in
Tables~\ref{tab:asr_en}--\ref{tab:asr_zh} are produced by us under
the same normaliser as the AmphionASR column.

\paragraph{Hotword conditioning setup.}
\label{subsec:hotword-setup}
Two protocols are evaluated on CommonVoice 17 ZH/EN hotword splits
with prompt-size sweep $k\!\in\!\{0, 5, 10, 15, 20\}$.
\emph{Oracle prompt}: ground-truth hotwords padded to $k$ entries
with random distractors, providing an upper bound on prompt-driven
biasing without testing the retriever.
\emph{Retrieve client}: the hotword list is constructed by the
parameter-free retrieval operator of Section~\ref{subsec:retrieve-algorithm}
from the full test-split pool, testing end-to-end biasing performance.
CER is reported for Mandarin and WER for English.

\paragraph{Target-speaker recognition setup.}
Three test sets are used: the internal \texttt{ts\_hw\_test}
($n_\text{pos}\!=\!6{,}227$ target-present, $n_\text{neg}\!=\!328$
target-absent, synthesised by the same pipeline as training but on
held-out speakers), Libri2Mix ($n\!=\!6{,}000$), and Libri3Mix
($n\!=\!9{,}000$). We report WER on positive samples, False Alarm
(FA), Miss, and Silence Match (SM\,$=$\,$1\!-\!$FA) on
\texttt{ts\_hw\_test}, and WER on LibriMix. Baselines are
Qwen3-ASR-0.6B, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B~\cite{qwen2025qwen3asr} and
Qwen3-Omni~\cite{xu2025qwen25omni}; all systems use the same
dual-audio prompt template.

\paragraph{Degradation robustness setup.}
\label{subsec:vitw-protocol}
We evaluate on Voices-in-the-Wild-Bench~\cite{xie2026mega}, a
$5{,}000$-clip English/Mandarin benchmark covering seven atomic
acoustic degradations (additive noise, far-field propagation,
obstruction, echo/reverberation, recording coloration, electronic
distortion, transmission dropout) under both real-world recordings
(\emph{Real}) and spectrogram-level simulation (\emph{Sim}), plus
compound-degradation Mixed subsets --- $16$ subsets in total. WER
is reported under the same normalisers as
Tables~\ref{tab:asr_en}--\ref{tab:asr_zh}; baseline numbers for
eight comparison systems are reproduced from~\cite{xie2026mega}
and use the Mega-ASR normalisation pipeline rather than ours.

shared between the offline batch decoder and the online batched
inference server. Table~\ref{tab:eval} lists the test sets

sets, the silence-aware target-speaker metrics
(WER\,/\,FA\,/\,Miss\,/\,SM, Section~\ref{sec:tsasr}) on TS-ASR
test sets, and per-system WER on Voices-in-the-Wild-Bench
(Section~\ref{sec:noise}) are computed by a single evaluation
harness, so that all numbers in the paper come from the same

\paragraph{Closest-scale comparison: Qwen3-ASR-1.7B.}
We treat Qwen3-ASR-1.7B as the closest open-source comparison
because its parameter scale ($1.7$\,B) is in the same order as our
LLM-side budget ($4.3$\,B distinct) and because the encoder of
AmphionASR is initialised from the Qwen3-ASR audio tower
(Section~\ref{sec:model}). AmphionASR surpasses Qwen3-ASR-1.7B on
LibriSpeech test-clean ($1.55$\,\% vs.\ $1.61$\,\% WER) and on
AIShell-1 ($1.51$\,\% vs.\ $1.58$\,\% CER), and reaches identical
CER ($2.77$\,\%) on AIShell-2. On the remaining seven test sets
Qwen3-ASR-1.7B leads by between $0.2$ and $1.8$ absolute points.

\paragraph{FireRed-ASR2-LLM dominates on Mandarin.}
The recently released FireRed-ASR2-LLM~\cite{fireredasr2025}
achieves the lowest CER on five of the six Mandarin test sets
(AIShell-1 $0.65$\,\%, KeSpeech $3.19$\,\%, CV17~zh $4.34$\,\%,
WenetSpeech test-net $4.43$\,\%, test-meeting $4.49$\,\%); on
AIShell-2 the leader is Step-Audio~2 at $2.35$\,\% CER. On the
English side Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct leads on the two LibriSpeech
splits ($1.30$\,\% / $2.56$\,\% WER), Qwen3-ASR-1.7B on GigaSpeech
($8.68$\,\%) and Step-Audio~2 on CV17~en ($6.77$\,\%). AmphionASR
is the best of the ten systems on no single test set of either
table, and trails the best Mandarin system (FireRed-ASR2-LLM) by
between $0.40$ and $3.80$ CER points across the six Mandarin
columns.

\paragraph{AmphionASR-specific observations.}
On the Mandarin grid AmphionASR reaches single-digit CER on every
reported test set; AIShell-1 at $1.51$\,\% is the headline data
point on read speech, AIShell-2 sits at $2.77$\,\%, the
spontaneous WenetSpeech splits at $5.75$\,\% / $6.90$\,\%, CV17~zh
at $5.94$\,\% and KeSpeech --- a multi-dialect Mandarin mixture
known to be the hardest condition for non-dialect-trained models
\cite{tang2021kespeech} --- at $6.99$\,\%, the worst single
Mandarin row. On the English grid the model is competitive on the
read-speech LibriSpeech splits ($1.55$\,\% / $3.62$\,\% WER on
test-clean / test-other) and on CV17~en ($8.34$\,\%); GigaSpeech
at $9.25$\,\% WER is the worst English row and corresponds to the
more spontaneous broadcast / interview domain inside that
benchmark \cite{chen2021gigaspeech}.

\paragraph{Code-switching and broader multilinguality.}
Mandarin--English code-switching, MLS, FLEURS and the
high-end-of-tail Mandarin sets (AIShell-3, MagicData, THCHS-30)
are not yet covered by our cross-system evaluation pack and are
therefore omitted from
Tables~\ref{tab:asr_en}--\ref{tab:asr_zh} in this revision; a
ten-baseline FLEURS multilingual sub-table and a TALCS
\cite{li2022talcs} code-switching sub-table are on the agenda for
the next revision.
