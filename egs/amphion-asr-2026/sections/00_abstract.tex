We present \textbf{AmphionASR}, an open SpeechLLM for Mandarin and
English transcription that is purpose-built for the failure modes that
dominate real-world deployments rather than open benchmark
leaderboards: rare named entities in domain-specific vocabularies,
overlapping speakers in multi-party audio, and noisy or far-field
acoustic conditions. AmphionASR couples a Whisper-style audio encoder
with a 4\,B-parameter pre-trained LLM through a lightweight modality
projector, exposing the LLM's prompt as the single interface through
which downstream applications inject all three forms of
side-information at inference time.

Three production-oriented capabilities anchor the design.
\emph{(i)~Hotword conditioning.} A comma-separated list of likely
terms is supplied in the prompt and the decoder is biased toward
them; with as few as five in-prompt hotwords, the CommonVoice~17
hotword test CER drops from $6.01$\% to $2.67$\% on Mandarin and
the WER from $7.68$\% to $4.69$\% on English (oracle prompt,
random padding). For deployment-scale pools we further describe a
parameter-free text-space retrieve-then-bias operator that
attains Recall@$K$ above $94\%$ on Mandarin from $K=10$ onwards.
\emph{(ii)~Target-speaker recognition.} Given a $3$--$5$\,s
reference utterance the model transcribes only the matching
speaker in mixed audio (\textbf{13.06}\,\% MER on our internal
hot+TS test set), with explicit anti-hallucination training that
keeps the decoder silent when the requested speaker is absent.
\emph{(iii)~Noise robustness.} Every target-speaker training example
already passes through reverberation, additive noise (MUSAN
\cite{snyder2015musan}, AudioSet road-traffic) and
competing-speaker interference; an additional noise-robustness SFT
stage extends the same augmentation to the plain-ASR branch and
gives AmphionASR its first controlled noise-robustness evaluation
grid.

On standard public benchmarks AmphionASR reaches
$\mathbf{1.85}$\,\% CER on AISHELL-1,
$\mathbf{1.82}$\,\% WER on LibriSpeech \texttt{test-clean} and
$\mathbf{7.48}$\,\% MER on the TALCS Mandarin--English
code-switching test. The released model is served behind a
batched vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm} endpoint and ships with the
inference client and the serving stack, targeting drop-in
deployment for production transcription pipelines. We also
discuss current gaps -- in particular weakness on the public
LibriMix multi-talker benchmarks and the residual gap to
Qwen3-ASR-1.7B \cite{qwen2025qwen3asr} on most non-MUSAN-speech
rows of the noise-robustness grid -- and outline the next
training iteration. Model weights and the vLLM-based serving stack will be
released at \url{https://github.com/amphionspace/AmphionASR}; the
training code is not part of this release
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
