We present \textbf{AmphionASR}, an open SpeechLLM for Mandarin and
English transcription that is purpose-built for the failure modes that
dominate real-world deployments rather than open benchmark
leaderboards: rare named entities in domain-specific vocabularies,
overlapping speakers in multi-party audio, and noisy or far-field
acoustic conditions. AmphionASR bridges a Whisper-style audio encoder
with the Qwen3-4B-Instruct LLM through a lightweight modality
projector, exposing the LLM's prompt as the single interface through
which downstream applications inject all three forms of
side-information at inference time.

Three production-oriented capabilities anchor the design.
\emph{(i)~Hotword conditioning.} A comma-separated list of likely
terms is supplied in the prompt and the decoder is biased toward
them; with as few as five in-prompt hotwords, CommonVoice~17 CER
drops from $6.57$\% to $2.83$\% on Mandarin and WER from $9.65$\% to
$4.89$\% on English.
\emph{(ii)~Target-speaker recognition.} Given a $3$--$5$\,s reference
utterance the model transcribes only the matching speaker in mixed
audio (\textbf{14.29}\,\% MER on our internal hot+TS test set), with
explicit anti-hallucination training that keeps the decoder silent
when the requested speaker is absent.
\emph{(iii)~Noise robustness.} Every target-speaker training cut
passes through reverberation, additive noise (MUSAN
\cite{snyder2015musan}, AudioSet road-traffic) and competing-speaker
interference, so deployments on noisy, overlapped or far-field audio
inherit the same robustness without bespoke acoustic adaptation.

On standard public benchmarks the v3 checkpoint reaches
$\mathbf{1.80}$\,\% CER on AISHELL-1,
$\mathbf{7.17}$\,\% WER on LibriSpeech \texttt{test-clean} and
$\mathbf{14.99}$\,\% MER on the TALCS Mandarin--English
code-switching test. The released model is served behind a batched
vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm} endpoint and ships with both the inference
client and the supervised fine-tuning recipe, targeting drop-in
deployment for production transcription pipelines. We also discuss
current gaps -- in particular weakness on the public LibriMix
multi-talker benchmarks and the absence of an explicit plain-ASR
noise curriculum -- and outline the next training iteration. Weights
and code will be released at
\url{https://github.com/amphionspace/AmphionASR}.
