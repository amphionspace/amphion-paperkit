\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Overview}

AmphionASR follows the now-standard SpeechLLM template
\cite{ma2024slamasr, chu2023qwenaudio, tang2024salmonn} -- a speech
encoder, a small projector, and a frozen-then-fine-tuned LLM -- but is
specialised in three respects that we describe in this section:
(i)~the Qwen3-AuT audio tower and its Whisper-style preprocessing;
(ii)~a lightweight Linear--SwooshR--Linear projector inherited from the
Zipformer activation family \cite{yao2024zipformer}; and
(iii)~four learnable \emph{boundary prompt} vectors that mark the
audio--text boundary inside the LLM's input embedding stream.
The full computational graph is reproduced as the teaser
\Cref{fig:teaser} on the title page.

Component-level parameter counts are summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:params}. The total of $\approx\!4.74$\,B includes the
tied output projection; counting distinct parameters the model is
$\approx\!4.3$\,B.

\subsection{Audio Encoder: Qwen3-AuT}
\label{subsec:audio-encoder}

\paragraph{Front-end.}
Audio is resampled to 16\,kHz mono and passed through a Whisper-style
log-mel front-end (Table~\ref{tab:fe}): a 25\,ms FFT window with
10\,ms hop produces a $128$-bin log-mel spectrogram at 100\,Hz, and
segments are padded or truncated to a 30\,s frame budget (3000 mel
frames).

\begin{table}[t]
  \caption{Audio front-end configuration.}
  \label{tab:fe}
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \toprule
    Sampling rate    & 16{,}000\,Hz \\
    n\_fft           & 400 \\
    Hop length       & 160 (10\,ms step) \\
    Feature size     & 128 mel bins \\
    Chunk length     & 30\,s \\
    Max frames       & 3000 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Convolutional sub-sampler.}
A three-layer 2-D convolutional stack ($\text{conv2d1}$:
$1\!\to\!480$, $3\!\times\!3$; $\text{conv2d2}$: $480\!\to\!480$,
$3\!\times\!3$; $\text{conv2d3}$: $480\!\to\!480$, $3\!\times\!3$)
followed by a $7680\!\to\!1024$ linear projection reduces the time-frequency
plane to a 1024-dimensional sequence.

\paragraph{Transformer stack.}
The encoder body is a 24-layer Whisper-style pre-LN Transformer with
$d_{\text{model}}=1024$, $16$ attention heads and feed-forward width
$4096$ (GELU activation). The full configuration is reproduced in
Table~\ref{tab:enc}. A chunked-inference window of $800$ frames
(versus $50$ at training time) lifts the standard Whisper $30$\,s
ceiling and lets the encoder process long-form audio in constant
memory by sliding over windows.

  \label{tab:enc}

    Input feature           & 128-bin log-mel \\

\paragraph{Effective frame rate.}
Although the front-end produces mel frames at 100\,Hz, the conv stem and
internal sub-sampling reduce the encoder's output rate to roughly
$13$\,Hz (i.e.\ a $\approx 76.9$\,ms step), as derived from
\texttt{Qwen3AudioEncoderWrapper.get\_output\_lengths}. The projector
does not perform any further temporal sub-sampling.

\paragraph{Output projection stripped.}
The native Qwen3-AuT ships with a 2048-dimensional alignment head that
we remove (replaced with \texttt{nn.Identity()} in
\texttt{Qwen3AudioEncoderWrapper.\_strip\_proj}). The exposed encoder
output dimension is therefore the body width of $1024$.

\subsection{Multi-Modal Projector}
\label{subsec:projector}

The projector maps each $1024$-dimensional encoder frame to the LLM's
$2560$-dimensional hidden space. Architecturally it is a two-layer MLP:

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Proj}(\mathbf{h}) = \mathrm{Linear}_{2560 \to 2560}\Big(
      \mathrm{SwooshR}\big( \mathrm{Linear}_{1024 \to 2560}(\mathrm{Drop}_{0.1}(\mathbf{h})) \big) \Big),
  \label{eq:projector}
\end{equation}

with input dropout $0.1$ and the SwooshR activation function from the
Zipformer/Icefall family \cite{yao2024zipformer}:

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SwooshR}(x) = \log(1 + e^{x-1}) - 0.08\,x - 0.313261687.
  \label{eq:swooshr}
\end{equation}

The projector is implemented in pure PyTorch (no \texttt{k2} C++
extension at inference time), at the cost of about $9.2$\,M
parameters and a single dropout--linear--activation--linear stack. The
projector is the only fully-trainable module in Stage~1 (audio--text
alignment, Section~\ref{sec:training}).

\subsection{Language Model: Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507}
\label{subsec:llm}

The decoder is the Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 release of the Qwen3 family
\cite{qwen2025qwen3}. Its full configuration is summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:llm}. Two points are worth highlighting:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Long context.} The 256\,K (262{,}144) native context
        length carries over to AmphionASR; together with the
        encoder's sliding window this enables long-form transcription
        that does not rely on chunk stitching.
  \item \textbf{Tied output embedding.} The model uses input--output
        embedding tying, so the language-modelling head shares weights
        with the input embedding. The component count in
        Table~\ref{tab:params} is therefore an over-count by one
        embedding tensor; we report ``$\approx\!4.3$\,B distinct''
        alongside ``$\approx\!4.74$\,B total''.
\end{itemize}

The LLM weights show no LoRA, adapter or quantisation tensors in the
released checkpoint, indicating that the LLM participates in training
under full fine-tuning during Stages~2 and 3.

\subsection{Learnable Modality-Boundary Prompts}
\label{subsec:boundary-prompt}

A small but distinguishing design choice in AmphionASR is a
$4 \times 2560$ embedding table that overrides the input embedding of
four boundary tokens (Table~\ref{tab:bound}) at every position they
appear:

\begin{table}[t]
  \caption{Learnable boundary prompt embedding mapping.}
  \label{tab:bound}
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{lcc}
    \toprule
    Boundary token       & Token id   & Prompt index \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{<start\_text>}    & 151670     & 0 \\
    \texttt{<end\_text>}      & 151671     & 1 \\
    \texttt{<start\_speech>}  & 151672     & 2 \\
    \texttt{<end\_speech>}    & 151673     & 3 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Conceptually this is a soft-prompt mechanism in the spirit of
prompt-tuning, restricted to the four positions that mark a switch
between modalities; it adds about $10$\,k parameters but gives the LLM
an explicit signal of ``a span of speech tokens starts now''. The
boundary prompt embedding is trainable in all stages.

\subsection{Special Tokens and Chat Template}
\label{subsec:tokens}

In addition to the Qwen3 vocabulary, AmphionASR defines the five
special tokens listed in Table~\ref{tab:tokens}. The audio segments
are wrapped as \verb|<start_speech><speech><end_speech>|, where
\verb|<speech>| is the placeholder that the merge step
(Section~\ref{subsec:merge}) replaces with the projected audio
embedding sequence.

AmphionASR exposes the same model under two prompt conventions
(Table~\ref{tab:tasks}): the \emph{Swift} convention used by the
ms-swift training pipeline (and adopted in every released SFT and
GRPO recipe in this report) and the \emph{Train} convention bundled
with the legacy icefall-style training scripts. The two differ only
in line ordering, the colon-vs-period at the end of the main
instruction, the spacing around the hotword value, and whether the
language hint is rendered as a separate line. The inference client
must be invoked with the matching style; a mismatch causes the model
to either repeat hotwords verbatim or skip the language hint, with
measurable WER damage.

\paragraph{Target-speaker conditioning.}
TS-ASR is implemented entirely in the prompt and the chat template:
the enrollment utterance is supplied as a separate
\verb|{"type": "audio"}| OpenAI multi-modal content item, which the
chat template renders as a \verb|<start_speech><speech><end_speech>|
triplet, and the main instruction switches from ``Transcribe the
following audio.'' to ``Transcribe what this speaker says in the
following audio.''. The model itself is unchanged -- both audio
segments share the audio encoder + projector path and reach the LLM as
two ordered audio embedding spans (enrollment first, mixed audio
second). Anti-hallucination negative samples
(Section~\ref{subsec:tsasr-synth}) inherit the same template and use an
empty assistant response.

\paragraph{Hotword conditioning.}
Hotwords are inserted into the prompt as a single comma-separated line.
At training time the line is included with probability $0.8$; at
evaluation time the eval client pads each utterance to a target $K$
hotwords by mixing the ground-truth list with random distractors drawn
from the dataset's own hotword pool (Section~\ref{subsec:hotword-pool}).
No special token marks the hotword list, so the LLM must learn the
\verb|Hotwords:| literal as part of the prompt grammar.

\subsection{Forward Pass and Merge}
\label{subsec:merge}

Given a raw waveform $\mathbf{x}$ and a tokenised prompt, the forward
pass proceeds as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\mathbf{x} \to$ log-mel front-end $\to (B, T_\text{mel}, 128)$
        spectrogram;
  \item Audio encoder produces $(B, T', 1024)$ frame representations;
  \item Projector maps to $(B, T', 2560)$;
  \item Text branch yields $(B, L, 2560)$ token embeddings;
  \item A merge step performs three operations: (i)~replace each
        \verb|<speech>| placeholder with the corresponding audio
        embedding sequence in left-to-right order; (ii)~override the
        four boundary-token embeddings with the learnable prompt
        vectors; (iii)~apply a flip--pad--flip trick that realises
        left-padding semantics so that batched autoregressive decoding
        stays aligned;
  \item The merged token / audio embedding stream is fed to
        Qwen3-4B-Instruct for next-token prediction. Training loss is
        the standard causal-LM cross-entropy with audio positions
        masked from the loss; only assistant tokens contribute
        gradients.
\end{enumerate}

Formally, with
$\mathbf{H}_\text{audio} = \mathrm{Proj}(\mathrm{AudioEncoder}(\mathbf{x}_\text{mel}))$
and $\mathbf{H}_\text{text}$ the text-side embedding stream after
boundary-prompt override, the per-example training objective is

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_\text{ans}}
      \log p_\theta\!\left( y_t \,\Big|\, y_{<t},\, \mathbf{H}_\text{audio},\, \mathbf{H}_\text{text} \right),
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal{T}_\text{ans}$ is the set of token indices belonging to
the assistant turn.

\subsection{Inference and Engineering Notes}
\label{subsec:inference}

A few engineering decisions are worth recording for reproducibility:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Pure-PyTorch SwooshR.} The activation in
        Equation~\ref{eq:swooshr} is reimplemented in PyTorch so the
        inference graph does not depend on any external C++ runtime.
  \item \textbf{Long-form audio.} The encoder is wrapped to process
        audio beyond Whisper's 30\,s budget by running per-utterance
        chunked inference, accommodating variable-length inputs.
  \item \textbf{KV-cache.} Generation feeds only the input embeddings
        and attention mask to the LLM; the audio encoder and the
        merge step are skipped once the KV-cache is populated, so
        per-step decoding cost is identical to a text-only LLM.
  \item \textbf{Pluggable encoder.} The audio encoder is loaded
        through a small factory that supports the Qwen3 audio
        encoder family as well as a Zipformer encoder
        \cite{yao2024zipformer}; the v3 checkpoint uses Qwen3-AuT,
        but the same code path can host a Zipformer for ablation.
  \item \textbf{Backward-compatible state-dicts.} A migration
        routine silently remaps weights from earlier checkpoints into
        the wrapped encoder namespace, so previous releases can be
        loaded without manual conversion.
  \item \textbf{Serving.} The released model is served via vLLM
        \cite{kwon2023vllm} as a batched HTTP endpoint; greedy
        decoding is the default for production traffic.

\end{itemize}
