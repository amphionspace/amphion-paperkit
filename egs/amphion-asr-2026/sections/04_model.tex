\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Overview}

AmphionASR follows the now-standard SpeechLLM template
\cite{ma2024slamasr, chu2023qwenaudio, tang2024salmonn} -- an audio
encoder, a small modality projector and a frozen-then-fine-tuned
LLM -- but is specialised in three respects that we describe in this
section: (i)~a Whisper-style audio encoder initialised from the
Qwen3-ASR audio tower; (ii)~a lightweight two-layer projector that
uses the SwooshR activation \cite{yao2024zipformer}; and (iii)~four
learnable \emph{modality-boundary prompts} that mark the audio--text
boundary inside the LLM's input-embedding stream. The full
computational graph is shown in \Cref{fig:architecture}.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/architecture.pdf}
  \caption{AmphionASR architecture. The audio branch (left) maps a
    16\,kHz waveform through a Whisper-style log-mel front-end, a
    convolutional sub-sampler, a 24-layer Whisper-style Transformer
    encoder and a lightweight modality projector into the LLM's
    2560-dimensional hidden space. The text branch (right) tokenises
    the chat template and replaces four special boundary tokens with
    learnable prompt vectors. The two streams meet at a merge step
    that substitutes the \texttt{<speech>} placeholder with the
    audio embedding sequence, after which Qwen3-4B-Instruct performs
    next-token prediction on the assistant span only.}
  \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Audio Encoder}
\label{subsec:audio-encoder}

The encoder is a 24-layer Whisper-style pre-LN Transformer
initialised from the Qwen3-ASR audio tower
\cite{qwen2025qwen3asr}. We describe its front-end, sub-sampling
stem and Transformer body in turn.

\paragraph{Front-end.}
Audio is resampled to 16\,kHz mono and passed through a Whisper-style
log-mel front-end (Table~\ref{tab:fe}): a 25\,ms FFT window with
10\,ms hop produces a $128$-bin log-mel spectrogram at 100\,Hz, and
segments are padded or truncated to a 30\,s frame budget (3000 mel
frames).

\begin{table}[t]
  \caption{Audio front-end configuration.}
  \label{tab:fe}
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \toprule
    Sampling rate    & 16{,}000\,Hz \\
    n\_fft           & 400 \\
    Hop length       & 160 (10\,ms step) \\
    Feature size     & 128 mel bins \\
    Chunk length     & 30\,s \\
    Max frames       & 3000 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Convolutional sub-sampler.}
Three stacked $3\!\times\!3$ 2-D convolutions ($1\!\to\!480$,
$480\!\to\!480$, $480\!\to\!480$ channels) followed by a
$7680\!\to\!1024$ linear projection reduce the time-frequency plane
to a 1024-dimensional sequence.

\paragraph{Transformer stack.}
The encoder body is a 24-layer Whisper-style pre-LN Transformer with
$d_{\text{model}}=1024$, $16$ attention heads and feed-forward width
$4096$ (GELU activation). The full configuration is reproduced in
Table~\ref{tab:enc}. A chunked-inference window of $800$ frames
(versus $50$ at training time) lifts the standard Whisper $30$\,s
ceiling and lets the encoder process long-form audio in constant
memory by sliding over windows.

\paragraph{Effective frame rate.}
Although the front-end produces mel frames at 100\,Hz, the
convolutional stem and the encoder's internal sub-sampling reduce the
output rate to roughly $13$\,Hz (i.e.\ a $\approx\!76.9$\,ms step).
The projector does not perform any further temporal sub-sampling.

\paragraph{Stripped alignment head.}
The audio tower as released ships with a 2048-dimensional alignment
head that we remove during initialisation. The exposed encoder output
dimension is therefore the Transformer body width of $1024$, which
the projector then maps directly into the LLM's hidden space.

\subsection{Modality Projector}
\label{subsec:projector}

The projector maps each $1024$-dimensional encoder frame to the LLM's
$2560$-dimensional hidden space. Architecturally it is a two-layer MLP
with an input dropout of $0.1$:

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Proj}(\mathbf{h}) = \mathrm{Linear}_{2560 \to 2560}\Big(
      \mathrm{SwooshR}\big( \mathrm{Linear}_{1024 \to 2560}(\mathbf{h}) \big) \Big),
  \label{eq:projector}
\end{equation}

where the SwooshR activation is the one introduced with the
Zipformer encoder \cite{yao2024zipformer}:

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SwooshR}(x) = \log(1 + e^{x-1}) - 0.08\,x - 0.313261687.
  \label{eq:swooshr}
\end{equation}

The projector contributes about $9.2$\,M parameters and is the only
fully-trainable module in Stage~1 (audio--text alignment,
Section~\ref{sec:training}).

\subsection{Language Model}
\label{subsec:llm}

The decoder is Qwen3-4B-Instruct (the Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 release
of the Qwen3 family \cite{qwen2025qwen3}). Its full configuration is
summarised in Table~\ref{tab:llm}. Two points are worth highlighting:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Long context.} The 256\,K (262{,}144) native context
        length carries over to AmphionASR; together with the
        encoder's sliding window this enables long-form transcription
        that does not rely on chunk stitching.
  \item \textbf{Tied output embedding.} The model uses input--output
        embedding tying, so the language-modelling head shares weights
        with the input embedding. The component count in
        Table~\ref{tab:params} is therefore an over-count by one
        embedding tensor; we report ``$\approx\!4.3$\,B distinct''
        alongside ``$\approx\!4.74$\,B total''.
\end{itemize}

published weight files contain no separate LoRA, adapter or
quantisation tensors.

\subsection{Learnable Modality-Boundary Prompts}
\label{subsec:boundary-prompt}

A small but distinguishing design choice in AmphionASR is a
$4 \times 2560$ embedding table that overrides the input embedding of
four boundary tokens (Table~\ref{tab:bound}) at every position they
appear:

\begin{table}[t]
  \caption{Learnable boundary prompt embedding mapping.}
  \label{tab:bound}
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{lcc}
    \toprule
    Boundary token       & Token id   & Prompt index \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{<start\_text>}    & 151670     & 0 \\
    \texttt{<end\_text>}      & 151671     & 1 \\
    \texttt{<start\_speech>}  & 151672     & 2 \\
    \texttt{<end\_speech>}    & 151673     & 3 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Conceptually this is a soft-prompt mechanism in the spirit of
prompt-tuning, restricted to the four positions that mark a switch
between modalities; it adds about $10$\,k parameters but gives the LLM
an explicit signal of ``a span of speech tokens starts now''. The
boundary prompt embedding is trainable in all stages.

\subsection{Special Tokens and Chat Template}
\label{subsec:tokens}

In addition to the Qwen3 vocabulary, AmphionASR defines the five
special tokens listed in Table~\ref{tab:tokens}. The audio segments
are wrapped as \verb|<start_speech><speech><end_speech>|, where
\verb|<speech>| is the placeholder that the merge step
(Section~\ref{subsec:merge}) replaces with the projected audio
embedding sequence.

  \small

    \midrule
    \texttt{<speech>}        & 151669 & audio placeholder, replaced by audio embeddings \\
    \texttt{<start\_text>}    & 151670 & text-segment start; embedding overridden \\
    \texttt{<end\_text>}      & 151671 & text-segment end \\

    \texttt{<end\_speech>}    & 151673 & speech-segment end \\

Each task is exposed to the LLM as a short instruction line followed
by the audio placeholder (Table~\ref{tab:tasks}). The language hint
and the hotword list are written as additional lines after the
instruction; the target-speaker variant prepends an enrollment audio
block before the main audio. The same prompt template is used at
training time and at inference time; a mismatch between the two
causes the model to either repeat hotwords verbatim or skip the
language hint, with measurable WER damage.

\paragraph{Target-speaker conditioning.}
TS-ASR is implemented entirely in the prompt and the chat template:
the enrollment utterance is supplied as an additional audio content
item that the chat template renders as a separate
\verb|<start_speech><speech><end_speech>| triplet, and the main
instruction switches from ``\textit{Transcribe the following
audio.}'' to ``\textit{Transcribe what this speaker says in the
following audio.}''. The model itself is unchanged -- both audio
segments share the audio encoder and projector path and reach the
LLM as two ordered audio-embedding spans (enrollment first, mixed
audio second). Anti-hallucination negative samples
(Section~\ref{subsec:tsasr-synth}) inherit the same template with an
empty assistant response.

\paragraph{Hotword conditioning.}
Hotwords are inserted into the prompt as a single comma-separated line.
At training time the line is included with probability $0.8$; at
evaluation time the eval client pads each utterance to a target $K$
hotwords by mixing the ground-truth list with random distractors drawn
from the dataset's own hotword pool (Section~\ref{subsec:hotword-pool}).
No special token marks the hotword list, so the LLM must learn the
\verb|Hotwords:| literal as part of the prompt grammar.

\subsection{Forward Pass and Merge}
\label{subsec:merge}

Given a raw waveform $\mathbf{x}$ and a tokenised prompt, the forward
pass proceeds as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\mathbf{x} \to$ log-mel front-end $\to (B, T_\text{mel}, 128)$
        spectrogram;
  \item the audio encoder produces $(B, T', 1024)$ frame
        representations;
  \item the projector maps each frame to $(B, T', 2560)$;
  \item the text branch yields $(B, L, 2560)$ token embeddings;
  \item a merge step performs two operations: (i)~replace each
        \verb|<speech>| placeholder with the corresponding audio
        embedding sequence in left-to-right order, and (ii)~override
        the four boundary-token embeddings with the learnable prompt
        vectors. Batched decoding uses left-padding semantics so that
        autoregressive generation stays aligned across the batch.
  \item the merged token / audio embedding stream is fed to the LLM
        for next-token prediction. Training loss is the standard
        causal-LM cross-entropy with audio positions masked from the
        loss; only assistant tokens contribute gradients.
\end{enumerate}

Formally, with
$\mathbf{H}_\text{audio} = \mathrm{Proj}(\mathrm{AudioEncoder}(\mathbf{x}_\text{mel}))$
and $\mathbf{H}_\text{text}$ the text-side embedding stream after
boundary-prompt override, the per-example training objective is

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_\text{ans}}
      \log p_\theta\!\left( y_t \,\Big|\, y_{<t},\, \mathbf{H}_\text{audio},\, \mathbf{H}_\text{text} \right),
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal{T}_\text{ans}$ is the set of token indices belonging to
the assistant turn.

\subsection{Inference}
\label{subsec:inference}

Three properties of the inference path are worth recording:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Long-form audio.} The encoder runs per-utterance
        chunked inference over the 800-frame window of
        Table~\ref{tab:enc}, lifting the 30\,s Whisper budget while
        keeping memory cost constant in the input duration.
  \item \textbf{KV-cache during decoding.} After the prompt is
        processed, the audio encoder and the merge step are skipped
        on subsequent decoding steps, so per-step cost matches that
        of a text-only LLM of the same size.
  \item \textbf{Serving.} The released model is served via
        vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm} as a batched HTTP endpoint;
        greedy decoding is the default for production traffic.

\end{itemize}
