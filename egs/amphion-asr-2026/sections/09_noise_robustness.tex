\section{Noise Robustness}
\label{sec:noise}

\subsection{What the SFT Pipeline Already Sees}

Stages~1--3 of the SFT recipe (Section~\ref{sec:training}) do not
include a dedicated ``noise-robustness'' fine-tuning stage, but
every TS-ASR training mixture (about $3$\,M cuts; roughly half of
the multi-task training stream) already passes through the
augmentation pipeline of Section~\ref{subsec:tsasr-synth}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Reverberation.} Each TS-ASR sample is convolved
        with an SLR26 / SLR28 RIR with probability $0.5$
        \cite{ko2017studyrir}, truncated to the first $1.5$\,s
        (early-reflection regime).
  \item \textbf{Additive noise.} An independently sampled
        background segment (MUSAN \cite{snyder2015musan} noise /
        music or AudioSet road-traffic noise, weighted $0.30$ /
        $0.20$ / $0.50$) is mixed at
        $\mathrm{SNR}\!\sim\!\mathrm{Uniform}(0, 25)$\,dB and the
        result is peak-normalised.
  \item \textbf{Speaker interference.} Each target waveform is
        mixed with $K\!\in\!\{1, 2\}$ same-language interferers at
        $\mathrm{SNR}\!\sim\!\mathcal{N}(5, 7^2)$\,dB clipped to
        $[-5, 20]$, with overlap fraction
        $\rho\!\sim\!\mathcal{N}(0.35, 0.2^2)$ in $[0.1, 1.0]$.
  \item \textbf{Background-only / silence inputs.} The noise-only
        negative sub-type (Section~\ref{subsec:tsasr-synth},
        $\sim$$2$--$5$\,\% of cuts) consists of a real enrollment
        paired with a $3$--$15$\,s noise-only mixed channel and an
        empty target transcript, explicitly teaching the decoder
        to stay silent when no speech is present.
\end{itemize}

This means that any apparent ``noise robustness'' on the standard
ASR benchmarks of Section~\ref{sec:experiments} from
Stages~1--3 alone would be an incidental side-effect of the
TS-ASR data pipeline rather than the result of an explicitly
designed noise curriculum, with two caveats that the next
subsection makes explicit.

\subsection{Noise-Robustness SFT Stage}
\label{subsec:nrsft}

The released AmphionASR checkpoint extends Stage~3 with a
\emph{noise-robustness SFT} stage in which the augmentation
pipeline of Section~\ref{subsec:tsasr-synth} is also applied to
the plain-ASR branch. The exact data mix and schedule of this
stage are documented in the model card released alongside this
report; this section reports its evaluation on a controlled
noise-robustness grid against the pre-noise-robustness checkpoint
as an ablation.

We construct $11$ degraded splits by applying a single distortion
operator to each of the two CommonVoice~17 clean tests
(\texttt{cv-en-test}, $n=16{,}402$; \texttt{cv-zh-test},
$n=10{,}647$):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{$6$ additive-noise splits} at
        SNR$\,\sim\,\mathrm{Uniform}(5, 20)$\,dB:
        AudioSet ESC, AudioSet traffic, DNS challenge, MUSAN music,
        MUSAN noise, MUSAN speech.
  \item \textbf{$4$ room-impulse-response splits}: RIR-Mega,
        SLR26, SLR28-real, SLR28-sim.
  \item \textbf{$1$ overlapping-noise split}: WHAM (free-style
        ambient).
\end{itemize}
The distortion operator is identical across systems; the WER
values are otherwise computed with the same Whisper normaliser
(English) / Mandarin normaliser (Mandarin) as
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsection{Analysis}

\paragraph{NR SFT closes the gap on competing-speaker noise.}
The largest single improvement from the noise-robustness SFT
stage is on the MUSAN-speech split, where the WER (CER) drops
from $41.19$\,\% to $18.74$\,\% on English and from $21.45$\,\%
to $9.45$\,\% on Mandarin -- $22.5$ and $12.0$ absolute points
respectively, and from above to below the corresponding
Qwen3-ASR-1.7B number on that split. On the other ten splits
AmphionASR yields small but consistent gains over the no-NR-SFT
ablation (typically $0.1$--$1.0$ absolute points), so the gross
effect of the noise-robustness SFT stage is concentrated on the
competing-speaker condition.

\paragraph{Residual gap to Qwen3-ASR-1.7B.}
Qwen3-ASR-1.7B remains the best system on most non-MUSAN-speech
rows, by margins of $0.1$ to $3.3$ absolute points. The gap
reflects that Qwen3-ASR-1.7B was trained with a substantially
larger and more diverse noise / RIR augmentation programme than
the noise-robustness SFT stage in this release. Closing this
residual is on the agenda for the next iteration;
Table~\ref{tab:noise_robustness} is the first controlled
noise-robustness evaluation we release for AmphionASR rather than
a head-to-head winner.

\paragraph{Distortion-type asymmetry.}
The two language columns track each other closely on every row of
Table~\ref{tab:noise_robustness} except MUSAN-speech, where the
English column starts substantially higher than the Mandarin
column ($41.19$ vs.\ $21.45$\,\% on the no-NR-SFT ablation) and
the released AmphionASR reduces both into the same single-digit /
mid-teens regime. We do not attribute this asymmetry further; a
controlled decomposition across distortion types is on the agenda
for the next iteration.
