\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

This section consolidates cross-cutting observations that did not fit
naturally inside any single capability section.

\subsection{Worst-Slice WER on the Headline Grid}

In the English grid of Table~\ref{tab:asr_en} the worst single
point is GigaSpeech at $9.45$\,\% WER -- a more spontaneous
broadcast / interview test set than the LibriSpeech / FLEURS /
CommonVoice rows around it. FLEURS-en, which earlier revisions of
this report flagged as a $30$\,\%-WER failure mode, now sits at
$5.53$\,\% WER, aligned with the other read-speech English
benchmarks. On the Mandarin grid (Table~\ref{tab:asr_zh}) the
worst point is KeSpeech at $6.45$\,\% CER, corresponding to the
multi-dialect mixture inside that benchmark. We treat the
GigaSpeech and KeSpeech rows as the natural residual on read-style
and dialect-style data respectively rather than as failure modes,
and reserve a controlled error-type breakdown for the next
iteration.

\subsection{Hotword Curve: Oracle vs.\ Retrieve Operating Points}
\label{subsec:why-five}

Section~\ref{sec:hotwords} reports two distinct operating points
along the prompt-driven hotword axis. In the \emph{oracle prompt}
protocol (random padding), the prompt-size that minimises CER /
WER on CommonVoice~17 is around five (Mandarin) or ten (English),
with a small non-monotonic upturn past those points; the upturn is
the gap between random padding at evaluation time and hard-negative
padding during Stage~3 training, but is small enough that a single
operating point in the $5$--$10$ range covers both languages. In
the \emph{retrieve client} protocol the operating point is fixed
by the retriever's own discriminative behaviour: Mandarin saturates
near Recall@$K \approx 95\%$ from $K = 10$ onwards, and English
plateaus near Recall@$K \approx 78\%$ at $K = 20$. The
Mandarin / English asymmetry is the difference between the pinyin
and content-word matching channels at the same pool size; a
mechanistic decomposition into recall versus false-trigger
contributions is not yet available and is left to future work.

\subsection{Libri2Mix vs Internal TS-ASR}

The gap between $13.06$\,\% MER on the internal target-speaker
test and $66.14$\,\% WER on Libri2Mix is the most important open
issue in this release. The underlying cause has not been diagnosed
and a controlled ablation against the LibriMix protocol is on the
agenda for the next training iteration
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Inherited Failure Modes}

LLM-based ASR is known to inherit characteristic LLM failure modes
\cite{radford2023whisper, chu2023qwenaudio}; the next release will
measure how strongly each of the following manifests in
AmphionASR:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Repetition loops on long silences or stationary noise. The
        noise-only negative branch of the TS-ASR pipeline
        (Section~\ref{subsec:tsasr-synth}) explicitly trains the
        model to terminate on silence under the target-speaker
        prompt. The noise-robustness SFT stage of
        Section~\ref{subsec:nrsft} extends this exposure to the
        plain-ASR branch with a controlled noise grid; a
        per-condition qualitative breakdown of residual
        repetition-loop behaviour is left to future work.
  \item Language drift on Mandarin--English code-switching, where
        short English phrases inside a Mandarin utterance can be
        ``corrected'' into Mandarin transcriptions of
        similar-sounding words.
  \item Occasional over-bias on supplied hotwords when the audio
        does not actually contain the term (see
        Section~\ref{subsec:why-five}).
\end{itemize}
