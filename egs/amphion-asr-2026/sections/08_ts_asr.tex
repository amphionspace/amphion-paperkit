\paragraph{Strong positive-WER improvement over the open baselines.}
On the positive sub-slice of \texttt{ts\_hw\_test}, AmphionASR
reaches $12.56$\,\% WER, roughly $4.2\times$ below the strongest
open-source dual-audio baseline (Qwen3-Omni, $52.90$\,\%) and
about $6.7\times$ below Qwen3-ASR-0.6B. The improvement is
consistent on the public LibriMix tests: $66.33$\,\% on Libri2Mix
($2.0\times$ below Qwen3-Omni's $135.91$\,\%) and $86.00$\,\% on
Libri3Mix ($1.6\times$ below Qwen3-Omni's $138.41$\,\%). The
LibriMix WER values exceed $100$\,\% for the three open baselines
because their hypothesis on mixed-both audio contains the
transcripts of all speakers concatenated together (the WER is
$\text{insertions}/|\text{ref}| \approx 100$--$200$\,\% when the
reference is the target speaker only); none of the three open
systems exhibits any speaker selection. AmphionASR brings these
two columns under $100$\,\%, but with a larger Libri2Mix
$\to$ Libri3Mix degradation ($+19.67$ WER points) than the
open baselines ($\le 5$ points each), which suggests an
intermediate state where speaker selection is being attempted but
is not yet robust to interferer count.

\paragraph{LibriMix gap to the internal set.}
The Libri2Mix $/$ Libri3Mix WER values are substantially weaker
than the \texttt{ts\_hw\_test} positive WER, and this gap is
itself substantially larger than the $\text{Real}\!\to\!\text{Sim}$
gap observed on Voices-in-the-Wild-Bench
(Table~\ref{tab:vitw_bench}). The training synthesiser already
includes LibriSpeech speakers
(Table~\ref{tab:tsasr_sources}), so this is not a pure
speaker-coverage issue; the underlying cause has not been
diagnosed and a controlled ablation is left for the next training
iteration (also surfaced in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{False-alarm behaviour on the negative sub-slice.}
On the $n_\text{neg} = 328$ negative samples of
\texttt{ts\_hw\_test} (target speaker not present), all three
open-source baselines yield $\text{FA} = 100.00\%$ -- they always
emit a non-empty transcript even when the requested speaker is
absent. AmphionASR reduces FA to $98.17\%$ ($\text{SM} = 1.83\%$),
which constitutes a faint but non-trivial signal compared to the
baseline floor. The bulk of this signal comes from the
\emph{silence} sub-type (pure background, no speaker at all);
under the \emph{distractor} sub-type (only interfering speakers)
AmphionASR remains at $100\%$ FA, matching the open baselines. As
discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, the underlying cause
is that the training-time augmentation source for this stage --
the public Voices-in-the-Wild-2M corpus -- contains no
\texttt{(audio, empty-target)} pairs, so the model has no explicit
supervision for ``stay silent on this audio''. Closing the
\emph{negative\_distractor} gap requires extending the SFT mixture
with explicit anti-hallucination negatives, which is the planned
next step.

\paragraph{Hotwords + target speaker (combined).}
The released checkpoint supports hotword and target-speaker
prompting simultaneously: in the TS-ASR template
(Table~\ref{tab:tasks}), a hotword line is appended after the
speaker instruction, so the model jointly conditions on the
enrollment audio and the in-prompt biasing list. A dedicated
combined benchmark (TS-ASR with $k$ hotwords supplied) is in
preparation and will appear in a future revision.
