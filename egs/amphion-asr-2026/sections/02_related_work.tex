\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{End-to-end ASR.}
End-to-end ASR replaces the HMM-DNN pipeline with a single neural model
\cite{graves2006ctc, graves2012rnnt, chan2016las}. The Conformer
encoder \cite{gulati2020conformer} has become a near-universal
backbone, and self-supervised pre-training on unlabeled audio
\cite{baevski2020wav2vec2, hsu2021hubert} provides strong starting
points that transfer to ASR with limited supervision. The Zipformer
encoder \cite{yao2024zipformer} further improves accuracy and
training efficiency, and contributes the SwooshR activation that we
re-use in our projector (Section~\ref{subsec:projector}).

\paragraph{Large-scale weakly-supervised ASR.}
Whisper \cite{radford2023whisper} demonstrated that 680\,k hours of
noisy weakly-supervised data yield a model that is robust across
languages and recording conditions. Its encoder, in particular, has
become a popular off-the-shelf speech feature extractor for downstream
audio LLMs, and is the encoder we use in this work.

\paragraph{LLM-based and audio-LLM ASR.}
A growing line of work pairs a speech encoder with a pre-trained LLM
decoder. Qwen-Audio \cite{chu2023qwenaudio} and
SALMONN \cite{tang2024salmonn} established the basic recipe --
encoder, projector, frozen-then-fine-tuned LLM. SLAM-ASR
\cite{ma2024slamasr} sharpens this idea, showing that a single linear
projector trained over a frozen speech encoder and a frozen LLM is
enough to reach competitive LibriSpeech numbers. More recent technical
reports such as Step-Audio~2 \cite{stepaudio22025},
Kimi-Audio \cite{kimiaudio2025} and Qwen3-ASR
\cite{qwen2025qwen3asr} push the data scale and add features such as
in-prompt context biasing, multilingual support and streaming
inference. AmphionASR follows this template but is optimised
specifically for transcription quality together with hotword and
target-speaker capabilities, and is built on Qwen3-4B-Instruct
\cite{qwen2025qwen3} for an explicit cost--quality target.

\paragraph{Hotword / contextual biasing.}
Classical approaches inject biasing via finite-state transducer
contextual graphs or on-the-fly shallow fusion. End-to-end neural
approaches such as CLAS \cite{pundak2018deep} learn to attend to
embeddings of hotword n-grams jointly with the recognizer, which is
robust to out-of-vocabulary terms supplied at inference time. With
LLM-based ASR, a textual hotword list can simply enter the prompt; this
is the route taken by Qwen3-ASR Flash \cite{qwen2025qwen3asr} and by
AmphionASR (Section~\ref{sec:hotwords}).

\paragraph{Multi-talker and target-speaker ASR.}
Multi-talker ASR aims to transcribe overlapping speech; representative
end-to-end approaches include serialized output training
\cite{kanda2020serialized}. Target-speaker ASR is the related but
narrower task of transcribing one designated speaker, typically using a
reference utterance to derive a speaker embedding that conditions the
recognizer. We train this capability end-to-end through prompt
conditioning and report results both on our internal Mandarin set and
on the public LibriMix benchmarks \cite{cosentino2020librimix}
(Section~\ref{sec:tsasr}).

\paragraph{Code-switching ASR.}
Mandarin--English code-switching is a stress test for multilingual ASR.
TALCS \cite{li2022talcs} provides 587\,h of real Mandarin--English
classroom audio and is the standard public benchmark in the line; we
report TALCS test-set MER as one of our headline numbers.

\paragraph{Data infrastructure for SpeechLLMs.}
Building a multi-task SpeechLLM involves shuffling tens of millions of
heterogeneous training examples drawn from diverse audio corpora,
transcript annotations, augmentation pools and on-the-fly synthesis.
We rely on Lhotse~\cite{zelasko2021lhotse} for unified data
preparation, on Emilia~\cite{he2024emilia} (DNSMOS-filtered
\cite{reddy2021dnsmos}) as our primary in-the-wild Mandarin /
English source, on MUSAN~\cite{snyder2015musan} for additive noise
and music, and on the SLR26 / SLR28 RIR releases that accompany the
augmentation study of \cite{ko2017studyrir} for room-impulse-response
convolution. These choices match the practice of recent open
SpeechLLM releases and let us re-use the standard recipes shipped
in those projects.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
Compared with general-purpose audio LLMs, AmphionASR is narrower in
scope (transcription, hotwords, target speaker) and explicit about its
gaps (no dedicated degradation-robustness training in this version). Compared
with the open Qwen3-ASR-1.7B / 0.6B \cite{qwen2025qwen3asr}, our model
has a larger LLM decoder and is positioned to favour bilingual quality
and prompt-driven biasing rather than the broadest language coverage.
