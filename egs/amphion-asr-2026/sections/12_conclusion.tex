\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \textbf{AmphionASR}, an open-source, industrial-grade
SpeechLLM for personalised Mandarin and English speech recognition.
The model couples a Whisper-style 24-layer audio encoder
($\approx\!314$\,M parameters, initialised from the Qwen3-ASR audio
tower) to the Qwen3-4B-Instruct language model through a
lightweight modality projector and four learnable modality-boundary
prompts -- $\approx\!4.7$\,B parameters in total ($\approx\!4.3$\,B
distinct after input--output embedding tying) -- and is trained in
three stages: audio--text alignment, continue-SFT for Mandarin and
English transcription, and capability SFT that adds in-prompt
hotword conditioning and reference-utterance target-speaker
recognition. The released AmphionASR checkpoint additionally
completes a degradation-robustness SFT stage on Voice-in-the-Wild-2M~\cite{xie2026mega} that extends
augmentation pipeline to the plain-ASR branch and is evaluated on
Voices-in-the-Wild-Bench~\cite{xie2026mega}
(Section~\ref{sec:noise}). On Mandarin benchmarks AmphionASR
reaches single-digit CER on every reported set, with AIShell-1 at
$1.51$\,\% as the headline data point. On English it covers the
LibriSpeech, GigaSpeech and CommonVoice grid, with LibriSpeech
\texttt{test-clean} at $1.55$\,\% WER. With
as few as five in-prompt hotwords on the CommonVoice~17 hotword
splits, CER and WER drop by roughly half (oracle prompt).
A parameter-free text-space retrieval operator
(Section~\ref{subsec:retrieve-algorithm}) extends this prompt-driven
hotword behaviour to deployment-scale pools by reducing the
prompt to a per-utterance top-$K$ subset of a pool of size up to
$10^4$. On Voices-in-the-Wild-Bench~\cite{xie2026mega}, the
$5{,}000$-clip degradation-robustness suite, AmphionASR leads five of
sixteen subsets against eight publicly reported systems.

What still needs to be solved -- and is the explicit subject of
the next release -- is closing the gap on the public multi-talker
LibriMix benchmarks, narrowing the residual gap to
Mega-ASR~\cite{xie2026mega} on the Sim-Echo and Sim-Recording
subsets of Table~\ref{tab:vitw_bench}, re-running the eight
Voices-in-the-Wild-Bench baselines through our own normaliser to
remove the cross-system normalisation drift, and upgrading the
inference path with a measured streaming mode. The training pipeline is documented in
Sections~\ref{sec:data}--\ref{sec:training}.
