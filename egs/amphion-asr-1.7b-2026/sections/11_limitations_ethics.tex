\section{Limitations and Broader Impact}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Language scope.}
AmphionASR is optimised for Mandarin and English, with limited
Mandarin--English code-switching data.
The reported evidence does not support claims of broader multilingual
coverage.

\paragraph{Target-speaker generalisation.}
Target-speaker ASR is substantially weaker on the public LibriMix tests
than on the positive sub-slice of the in-house test set
(Table~\ref{tab:tsasr}).
The experiments do not identify the source of this gap.

This discrepancy limits conclusions about transfer across multi-talker
conditions.
In particular, the current evidence does not isolate corpus,
synthesis, or interferer-count effects.

\paragraph{Target-absent behaviour.}
The in-house target-speaker test set includes target-absent samples,
but this report does not provide false-alarm metrics for that subset.
Target-absent behaviour is therefore outside the evaluated evidence.

\paragraph{Degradation coverage.}
AmphionASR records the lowest WER on eight of the sixteen
Voice-in-the-Wild-Bench subsets.
The remaining conditions include real noise, mixed degradations, and
simulated transmission dropout.

The evaluation reports these residual gaps but does not provide a
controlled decomposition of their causes.

\paragraph{Retrieval scope.}
The hotword retriever is evaluated on Mandarin and English candidate
pools.
Its behaviour under other writing systems, dialectal pronunciations,
and longer audio has not been established.

\paragraph{Unmeasured generation failures.}
The evaluation does not systematically quantify repetition on long
silences, language drift in code-switched speech, or false insertion of
prompt-supplied hotwords.
These are known risks for LLM-based ASR, not measured AmphionASR
failure rates~\cite{radford2023whisper,chu2023qwenaudio}.

\paragraph{Latency and streaming.}
The evaluated system uses offline batched inference.
Streaming behaviour and first-token latency are not measured in this
report.

\subsection{Broader Impact}

\paragraph{Surveillance risk.}
Target-speaker ASR can reduce the effort required to transcribe a
designated person in multi-speaker audio.
This supports user-initiated transcription and accessibility, but it
can also enable surveillance without consent.

The intended use is transcription initiated by a participant with the
consent of all recorded parties.
Unconsented surveillance is outside the intended scope.

\paragraph{Bias.}
We do not report per-accent WER.
The aggregate results therefore do not establish comparable accuracy
across accents or dialects.

\paragraph{Data provenance.}
Public sources are used under their stated licenses
(Tables~\ref{tab:data_public}--\ref{tab:data_hotwords_eval}).
In-house sources are subject to in-house data-governance review.

\paragraph{LLM-assisted writing.}
Portions of this report were drafted with the assistance of large
language models.
The authors verified technical claims, numbers, and citations against
the corresponding primary sources.
