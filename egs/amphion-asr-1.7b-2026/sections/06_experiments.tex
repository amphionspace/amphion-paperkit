\paragraph{Normalization.}
For English benchmarks we apply the Whisper English text normaliser
\cite{radford2023whisper} to both reference and hypothesis before
computing WER. For Mandarin we apply a stripped-down normaliser that
removes punctuation, normalises full-width and half-width characters
and maps Arabic numerals to their Mandarin equivalents before CER.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare AmphionASR against eight recent open-source speech-LLM and
dedicated ASR systems, organised loosely by training objective:
audio-understanding and omni-modal LLMs --- Whisper-large-v3
\cite{radford2023whisper}, Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct \cite{kimiaudio2025},
Step-Audio~2 \cite{stepaudio22025}, MiniCPM-o \cite{minicpm2025},
MOSS-Audio \cite{moss2026audio} --- and dedicated ASR-oriented
LLM-based systems --- Qwen3-ASR-1.7B \cite{qwen2025qwen3asr},
FireRed-ASR2-LLM \cite{fireredasr2025}, and GLM-ASR-nano
\cite{glmasr2025}. The baseline columns in
Tables~\ref{tab:asr_en}--\ref{tab:asr_zh} are produced by us under the
same normaliser as the AmphionASR column.

\paragraph{Hotword conditioning setup.}
We evaluate two conditions on the CommonVoice ZH/EN hotword splits:
without retrieval and with retrieval, where the prompt receives the
top-$K$ candidates selected from the full test-split pool by the
operator of Section~\ref{subsec:hotword-rag}. CER is reported for
Mandarin and WER for English; entity B-WER and B-CER are used for
GigaSpeechBench.

\paragraph{Target-speaker ASR setup.}
Three test sets are used: an in-house target-speaker test set
(synthesised by the same pipeline as training but on held-out
speakers), Libri2Mix, and Libri3Mix. We report positive-slice WER
on all three sets.
Baselines are Qwen3-ASR-0.6B, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B~\cite{qwen2025qwen3asr}
and Qwen3-Omni~\cite{qwen2025qwen3omni}; all systems use the same
dual-audio prompt template.

\paragraph{Degradation robustness setup.}
We evaluate on Voice-in-the-Wild-Bench~\cite{xie2026mega}, a
$5{,}000$-clip English/Mandarin benchmark covering seven atomic
acoustic degradations (additive noise, far-field propagation,
obstruction, echo/reverberation, recording coloration, electronic
distortion, transmission dropout) under both real-world recordings
(\emph{Real}) and spectrogram-level simulation (\emph{Sim}), plus
compound-degradation Mixed subsets --- $16$ subsets in total. Baseline
numbers for eight comparison systems are reproduced
from~\cite{xie2026mega}.

\paragraph{Comparison with the Qwen3-ASR backbone.}
Qwen3-ASR-1.7B is the closest comparison because AmphionASR reuses its
audio encoder and language backbone.
AmphionASR leads on four of the ten test sets, while Qwen3-ASR-1.7B
leads on the remaining six by $0.13$--$0.85$ absolute points.

\paragraph{Comparison with other open systems.}
FireRed-ASR2-LLM~\cite{fireredasr2025} leads five Mandarin columns,
and Step-Audio~2 leads AIShell-2.
The English column leaders vary across Kimi-Audio, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B, and
Step-Audio~2.

AmphionASR does not lead any column in these two tables.
We therefore use the plain-ASR grid as a retention check rather than
as a state-of-the-art claim.

The highest AmphionASR errors occur on GigaSpeech and the WenetSpeech
meeting split, both of which contain more spontaneous speech than the
read-speech sets~\cite{chen2021gigaspeech}.
Their error composition is not analysed in this report.
We therefore report this variation without assigning a mechanism.
