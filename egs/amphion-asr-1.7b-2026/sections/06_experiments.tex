\paragraph{Normalization.}
For English benchmarks we apply the Whisper English text normalizer
\cite{radford2023whisper} to both reference and hypothesis before
computing WER. For Mandarin we apply a stripped-down normalizer that
removes punctuation, normalises full-width / half-width characters
and maps Arabic numerals to their Mandarin equivalents before CER.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare AmphionASR against eight recent open-source speech-LLM and
dedicated ASR systems, organised loosely by training objective:
audio-understanding / omni-modal LLMs --- Whisper-large-v3
\cite{radford2023whisper}, Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct \cite{kimiaudio2025},
Step-Audio~2 \cite{stepaudio22025}, MiniCPM-o \cite{minicpm2025},
MOSS-Audio \cite{moss2026audio} --- and dedicated ASR-oriented
LLM-based systems --- Qwen3-ASR-1.7B \cite{qwen2025qwen3asr},
FireRed-ASR2-LLM \cite{fireredasr2025}, and GLM-ASR-nano
\cite{glmasr2025}. The baseline columns in
Tables~\ref{tab:asr_en}--\ref{tab:asr_zh} are produced by us under the
same normaliser as the AmphionASR column.

\paragraph{Hotword conditioning setup.}
We evaluate two conditions on the CommonVoice ZH/EN hotword splits:
w/o RAG (no prompt hotwords) and w/ RAG, where the hotword list is the
top-$K$ candidates retrieved from the full test-split pool by the
retrieval operator of Section~\ref{subsec:hotword-rag}. CER is reported
for Mandarin and WER for English; entity B-WER / B-CER is used for
GigaSpeechBench.

\paragraph{Target-speaker recognition setup.}
Three test sets are used: an in-house target-speaker test set
(synthesised by the same pipeline as training but on held-out
speakers), Libri2Mix, and Libri3Mix. We report positive-slice WER
on all three sets.
Baselines are Qwen3-ASR-0.6B, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B~\cite{qwen2025qwen3asr}
and Qwen3-Omni~\cite{qwen2025qwen3omni}; all systems use the same
dual-audio prompt template.

\paragraph{Degradation robustness setup.}
We evaluate on Voice-in-the-Wild-Bench~\cite{xie2026mega}, a
$5{,}000$-clip English/Mandarin benchmark covering seven atomic
acoustic degradations (additive noise, far-field propagation,
obstruction, echo/reverberation, recording coloration, electronic
distortion, transmission dropout) under both real-world recordings
(\emph{Real}) and spectrogram-level simulation (\emph{Sim}), plus
compound-degradation Mixed subsets --- $16$ subsets in total. Baseline
numbers for eight comparison systems are reproduced
from~\cite{xie2026mega}.

\subsection{Evaluation Datasets and Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval-data}

All evaluations are dispatched through a unified evaluation harness,
shared between the offline batch decoder and the online batched
inference server, so that all numbers in the paper come from the same
metric implementation. Table~\ref{tab:eval} lists the test sets
reported in this paper.

\subsection{Plain ASR}
\label{subsec:plain-asr}

The headline contribution of AmphionASR is its four deployment
capabilities---hotword conditioning, target-speaker ASR, degradation
robustness, and whispered-speech ASR---reported in the sections that
follow. We report standard Mandarin and English ASR here only to
confirm that the capability-oriented SFT does not regress baseline
recognition: Tables~\ref{tab:asr_en}--\ref{tab:asr_zh} compare
AmphionASR against eight open-source systems on six Mandarin and four
English test sets.

\paragraph{Closest-scale comparison: Qwen3-ASR-1.7B.}
We treat Qwen3-ASR-1.7B as the closest open-source comparison because
its parameter scale ($1.7$\,B) matches our language backbone and
because the encoder of AmphionASR is initialised from the Qwen3-ASR
audio encoder. AmphionASR surpasses Qwen3-ASR-1.7B on LibriSpeech
test-clean ($1.59$\,\% vs.\ $1.61$\,\% WER) and test-other
($3.35$\,\% vs.\ $3.39$\,\%), on AIShell-1 ($1.34$\,\% vs.\ $1.58$\,\%
CER), and on AIShell-2 ($2.65$\,\% vs.\ $2.77$\,\%). On the remaining
six test sets Qwen3-ASR-1.7B leads by between $0.13$ and $0.85$
absolute points.

\paragraph{FireRed-ASR2-LLM dominates on Mandarin.}
The recently introduced FireRed-ASR2-LLM~\cite{fireredasr2025}
achieves the lowest CER on five of the six Mandarin test sets
(AIShell-1 $0.65$\,\%, KeSpeech $3.19$\,\%, CV~zh $4.34$\,\%,
WenetSpeech test-net $4.43$\,\%, test-meeting $4.49$\,\%); on
AIShell-2 the leader is Step-Audio~2 at $2.35$\,\% CER. On the
English side Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct leads on the two LibriSpeech
splits ($1.30$\,\% / $2.56$\,\% WER), Qwen3-ASR-1.7B on GigaSpeech
($8.68$\,\%) and Step-Audio~2 on CV~en ($6.77$\,\%). AmphionASR is the
best of the nine systems on no single test set of either table; on
Mandarin it trails the per-column leader by $0.30$--$2.52$ CER points
(FireRed-ASR2-LLM on five of the six columns, Step-Audio~2 on
AIShell-2).

\paragraph{AmphionASR-specific observations.}
On the Mandarin grid AmphionASR reaches single-digit CER on every
reported test set; AIShell-1 at $1.34$\,\% is the headline data point
on read speech, AIShell-2 sits at $2.65$\,\%, CV~zh at $5.21$\,\%,
KeSpeech~\cite{tang2021kespeech} (a multi-dialect Mandarin mixture) at
$5.71$\,\%, and the spontaneous WenetSpeech splits at $5.17$\,\% /
$6.30$\,\%, the latter being the worst Mandarin row. On the English
grid the model is competitive on the read-speech LibriSpeech splits
($1.59$\,\% / $3.35$\,\% WER on test-clean / test-other) and on CV~en
($7.87$\,\%); GigaSpeech at $9.53$\,\% WER is the worst English row and
corresponds to the more spontaneous broadcast / interview domain inside
that benchmark~\cite{chen2021gigaspeech}.
