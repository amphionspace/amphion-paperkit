\paragraph{Zero-shot Qwen3-Omni protocol.}
We evaluate Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct without task-specific
fine-tuning. Each request provides an enrollment utterance followed by
a mixture and uses the instruction in
Listing~\ref{lst:qwen3-omni-tsasr-prompt}. The prompt assigns the two
audio roles explicitly and requests
\mbox{\texttt{<NO\_TARGET\_SPEECH>}} when
the target is absent. This sentinel is deterministically mapped to an
empty hypothesis before scoring. Decoding is greedy and capped at
$200$ output tokens.

\paragraph{Comparison with open baselines.}
On the positive sub-slice of our in-house target-speaker test set,
AmphionASR reaches $13.02$\,\% WER, compared with $31.91$\,\% for
zero-shot Qwen3-Omni and $84.97$\,\% for Qwen3-ASR-0.6B.
On Libri2Mix and Libri3Mix, Qwen3-Omni obtains $72.58$\,\% and
$91.57$\,\% WER, while AmphionASR obtains $63.04$\,\% and
$85.04$\,\%, respectively.

The LibriMix WER values exceed $100$\,\% for the two Qwen3-ASR
baselines because their hypotheses
on mixed-both audio contain the transcripts of all speakers
concatenated together (the WER is
$\text{insertions}/|\text{ref}| \approx 100$--$200$\,\% when the
reference is the target speaker only). The instruction-enhanced
Qwen3-Omni baseline brings both public-set WERs below $100$\,\% without
task-specific fine-tuning.

AmphionASR brings both public columns below $100$\,\%, but its WER
increases by $22.00$ points from Libri2Mix to Libri3Mix.
The cause of this difference is not established here.

\paragraph{Target-absent silence behaviour.}
The in-house test set additionally contains $n_\text{neg}=328$
target-absent negatives, split evenly into a silence condition (no
speaker active, $164$ samples) and a distractor condition (an
interfering speaker active, $164$ samples).
On these negatives, both Qwen3-ASR baselines yield
$\text{FA}=100.00$\,\%. Zero-shot Qwen3-Omni lowers overall FA to
$42.07$\,\%, with $9.15$\,\% on silence and $75.00$\,\% on distractor
mixtures. Its positive-slice miss rate is $4.13$\,\%, so its WER and FA
should be read jointly. AmphionASR reaches an overall FA of
$35.67$\,\% and correctly stays silent on $64.33$\,\% of target-absent
samples.
As separated in Table~\ref{tab:tsasr}, the false-alarm rate is
$6.10$\,\% ($93.90$\,\% correct silence) in the silence condition and
$65.24$\,\% ($34.76$\,\% correct silence) in the distractor condition.
Most remaining false alarms therefore occur when another speaker is
active rather than when the input is silent.
The public Libri2Mix and Libri3Mix sets contain no target-absent
negatives, so FA is not applicable there.

These results support speaker-selection capability when the requested
speaker is present, and the in-house negatives provide a first
target-absent silence measurement. They do not establish competitiveness
with dedicated TS-ASR systems under a matched protocol. The available
experiments also do not isolate corpus, synthesis, or interferer-count
effects behind the in-house/public or silence/distractor gaps.
