\paragraph{Comparison with open baselines.}
On the positive sub-slice of our in-house target-speaker test set,
AmphionASR reaches $13.02$\,\% WER, compared with $52.90$\,\% for
Qwen3-Omni and $84.97$\,\% for Qwen3-ASR-0.6B.
On the public LibriMix tests, AmphionASR reaches $63.04$\,\% on
Libri2Mix and $85.04$\,\% on Libri3Mix.

The LibriMix WER values
exceed $100$\,\% for the three open baselines because their hypothesis
on mixed-both audio contains the transcripts of all speakers
concatenated together (the WER is
$\text{insertions}/|\text{ref}| \approx 100$--$200$\,\% when the
reference is the target speaker only); none of the three open systems
demonstrates speaker selection under this prompt and positive-slice
protocol.

AmphionASR brings both public columns below $100$\,\%, but its WER
increases by $22.00$ points from Libri2Mix to Libri3Mix.
The cause of this difference is not established here.

These results support speaker-selection capability when the requested
speaker is present. They do not evaluate target-absent silence
behaviour or establish competitiveness with dedicated TS-ASR systems.
The available experiments also do not isolate corpus, synthesis, or
interferer-count effects behind the gap between the in-house and public
mixtures.
