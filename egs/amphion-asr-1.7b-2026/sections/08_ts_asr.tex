\paragraph{Comparison with open baselines.}
On the positive sub-slice of our in-house target-speaker test set,
AmphionASR reaches $13.02$\,\% WER, compared with $52.90$\,\% for
Qwen3-Omni and $84.97$\,\% for Qwen3-ASR-0.6B.
On the public LibriMix tests, AmphionASR reaches $63.04$\,\% on
Libri2Mix and $85.04$\,\% on Libri3Mix.

The LibriMix WER values
exceed $100$\,\% for the three open baselines because their hypothesis
on mixed-both audio contains the transcripts of all speakers
concatenated together (the WER is
$\text{insertions}/|\text{ref}| \approx 100$--$200$\,\% when the
reference is the target speaker only); none of the three open systems
demonstrates speaker selection under this prompt and positive-slice
protocol.

AmphionASR brings both public columns below $100$\,\%, but its WER
increases by $22.00$ points from Libri2Mix to Libri3Mix.
The cause of this difference is not established here.

\paragraph{Target-absent silence behaviour.}
The in-house test set additionally contains $n_\text{neg}=328$
target-absent negatives, split evenly into a silence condition (no
speaker active, $164$ samples) and a distractor condition (an
interfering speaker active, $164$ samples).
On these negatives the three open baselines all yield
$\text{FA}=100.00$\,\%: with no target-absent silence supervision they
always emit a non-empty transcript even when the requested speaker is
absent. AmphionASR reduces the overall false-alarm rate to $35.67$\,\%,
i.e.\ it correctly stays silent on $64.33$\,\% of the target-absent
samples, providing a non-trivial improvement over the baseline floor.
The two sub-types behave very differently: on the silence condition
the false-alarm rate is only $6.10$\,\% ($93.90$\,\% correct silence),
whereas on the distractor condition it rises to $65.24$\,\%
($34.76$\,\% correct silence) -- when the target speaker is absent but
an interfering speaker is talking, the model frequently transcribes the
interferer instead of remaining silent.
This indicates that the model has learned the target-absent silence
objective in the easier silence condition, but distractor rejection
remains the limiting factor for target-speaker selection.
The public Libri2Mix and Libri3Mix sets contain no target-absent
negatives, so FA is not applicable there.

These results support speaker-selection capability when the requested
speaker is present, and the in-house negatives provide a first
target-absent silence measurement. They do not yet establish
competitiveness with dedicated TS-ASR systems, which we leave to future
work with matched-protocol baselines. The available experiments also do
not isolate corpus, synthesis, or interferer-count effects behind the
gap between the in-house and public mixtures, or behind the
silence-versus-distractor false-alarm gap.
