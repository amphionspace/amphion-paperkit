\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

This section consolidates cross-cutting observations that did not fit
naturally inside any single capability section.

\subsection{Worst-Slice WER on the Headline Grid}

In the English grid of Table~\ref{tab:asr_en} the worst single point
is GigaSpeech at $9.53$\,\% WER, a more spontaneous broadcast /
interview test set than the LibriSpeech and CommonVoice rows. On the
Mandarin grid (Table~\ref{tab:asr_zh}) the worst point is the
WenetSpeech meeting split at $6.30$\,\% CER. We treat these as the
natural residual on spontaneous / meeting-style data rather than as
failure modes, and reserve a controlled error-type breakdown for the
next iteration.

\subsection{Libri2Mix vs In-House TS-ASR}

The gap between $13.02$\,\% WER on the positive sub-slice of the
in-house target-speaker test set and $63.04$\,\% / $85.04$\,\% WER on
Libri2Mix / Libri3Mix (Table~\ref{tab:tsasr}) is the main
positive-sample open issue in this work. The underlying causes have
not been diagnosed and controlled ablations are on the agenda for the
next training iteration.

\subsection{Inherited Failure Modes}

LLM-based ASR is known to inherit characteristic LLM failure modes
\cite{radford2023whisper, chu2023qwenaudio}; the next iteration will
measure how strongly each of the following manifests in AmphionASR:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Repetition loops on long silences or stationary noise. The
        noise-only negative branch of the TS-ASR pipeline explicitly
        trains the model to terminate on silence under the
        target-speaker prompt; our model additionally
        extends this augmentation exposure to the plain-ASR branch
        through the degradation-robustness SFT stage of
        Section~\ref{sec:training}. A per-condition qualitative
        breakdown of residual repetition-loop behaviour is left to
        future work.
  \item Language drift on Mandarin--English code-switching, where
        short English phrases inside a Mandarin utterance can be
        ``corrected'' into Mandarin transcriptions of
        similar-sounding words.
  \item Occasional over-bias on supplied hotwords when the audio does
        not actually contain the term.
\end{itemize}
