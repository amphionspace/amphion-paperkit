\subsection{Hotword Conditioning}
\label{sec:hotwords}

\paragraph{Hotword pools.}
We evaluate the retriever under two pool regimes. For the
GigaSpeechBench vertical domains, we collect every hotword that the
benchmark annotates in each domain, deduplicate it, and build a
separate retrieval pool per domain and language (Mandarin / English);
these per-domain pools are small, ranging from a few hundred to a few
thousand entries. To stress the retriever at deployment-scale pool
sizes, we additionally build large CommonVoice-based pools: we use
Doubao-Seed-2.0-Lite~\cite{doubao2025seed} to annotate hotword
candidates on CommonVoice Mandarin and English, yielding $10{,}112$
Mandarin and $7{,}561$ English entries, and annotate extra hotwords
from GigaSpeech~\cite{chen2021gigaspeech} to fill the English pool to
$10{,}000$.

\paragraph{GigaSpeechBench vertical-domain entity recognition.}
GigaSpeechBench~\cite{tu2026gigaspeechbench} measures recognition of
domain entities---professional terms and named entities annotated in
each reference---rather than full-sentence accuracy. We follow the
benchmark protocol: on Mandarin domains the primary metric is
\textbf{biased CER} (B-CER), which counts substitution, deletion and
insertion errors only on entity tokens in the reference; on English
domains the counterpart is \textbf{biased WER} (B-WER). Each of the
twelve vertical domains (agriculture, AI technology, arts, biology,
e-commerce, engineering, entertainment, finance, humanities, law,
medicine, military) provides an in-the-wild test split, and w/ RAG
retrieves from the per-domain pool described above. Every baseline is
reported under plain ASR (no prompt hotwords). We add two AmphionASR
protocols on the same entity metric: w/o RAG (no prompt hotwords) and
w/ RAG ($K{=}50$, per-domain pool). Baseline numbers are reproduced
from the benchmark~\cite{tu2026gigaspeechbench}.

\paragraph{Entity-recognition analysis.}
Tables~\ref{tab:gsb_zh} and \ref{tab:gsb_en} list per-domain entity
error for every system plus the domain-average (AVG) column. Under
w/o RAG, AmphionASR reaches $23.18$\,\% AVG B-CER on Mandarin, worse
than Qwen3-ASR-1.7B ($18.63$\,\%). w/ RAG ($K{=}50$) closes this gap,
cutting AVG B-CER to $10.39$\,\% ($-55$\,\% relative) and AVG entity
B-WER to $9.35$\,\% ($-37$\,\% relative). On AVG, w/ RAG outperforms
every listed plain-ASR baseline on both English and Mandarin, edging
out FunASR-Realtime~\cite{an2025funasr} on Mandarin ($10.39$ vs.\
$10.91$). Per domain, retrieval beats Qwen3-ASR-1.7B on eleven of
twelve Chinese domains and on all twelve English domains. The largest
gains over the w/o RAG row are on ENT ($33.94 \to 10.01$ B-CER) and
AIT ($47.49 \to 19.10$ B-CER). Residual gaps to FunASR-Realtime
concentrate on FIN ($3.05$ vs.\ $2.45$ B-CER) and MIL ($8.28$ vs.\
$2.41$ B-CER). On English, retrieval brings seven of twelve domains to
single-digit entity B-WER, while AIT-EN remains the highest English
column at $20.93$\,\%.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
w/ RAG attains Recall@$50$ above $94$\,\% on both CommonVoice
languages, reducing entity B-CER / B-WER from $9.27$\,\% / $28.93$\,\%
(w/o RAG) to $2.30$\,\% / $9.81$\,\% (w/ RAG).
