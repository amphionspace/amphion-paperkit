\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \textbf{AmphionASR}, an industrial-grade SpeechLLM for
Mandarin and English speech recognition, built on the Qwen3-ASR-1.7B
audio encoder and language backbone. Capability building is a
three-stage all-linear LoRA SFT recipe initialised from
Qwen3-ASR-1.7B over the eight training sets of
Section~\ref{sec:data}.
On Mandarin benchmarks AmphionASR reaches single-digit CER on
every reported set, with AIShell-1 at $1.34$\,\% as the headline data
point. On English it covers the LibriSpeech, GigaSpeech and
CommonVoice grid, with LibriSpeech \texttt{test-clean} at $1.59$\,\%
WER. A dual-encoder retrieval operator extends hotword conditioning
to deployment-scale pools by reducing the prompt to a per-utterance
top-$K$ subset, attaining Recall@$50$ above $94$\,\% on CommonVoice and
strong entity B-WER / B-CER on GigaSpeechBench vertical
domains~\cite{tu2026gigaspeechbench}. On target-speaker
recognition the model reaches $13.02$\,\% WER on the positive
sub-slice of our in-house target-speaker test set, roughly
$4.1\times$ below the strongest open-source dual-audio baseline. On
Voice-in-the-Wild-Bench the model is competitive against eight
publicly reported systems, and on Mandarin wEar whisper test audio it
reaches $0.58$\,\% CER.

What still needs to be solved -- and is the explicit subject of the
next iteration -- is closing the gap on the public multi-talker
LibriMix benchmarks, narrowing the residual gaps to Mega-ASR on the
Real Noise, Real Mixed, and Sim Transmission dropout subsets of
Table~\ref{tab:vitw_bench}, and extending the training mixture with
explicit anti-hallucination negatives for target-speaker deployment.
The training pipeline is documented in
Sections~\ref{sec:data}--\ref{sec:training}.
