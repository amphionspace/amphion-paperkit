Modern speech recognition has evolved through several stages.
Hybrid HMM--DNN systems~\cite{hinton2012deep} dominated for decades by
combining separate acoustic, lexicon, and language models, but relied
on hand-engineered components and a multi-stage decoding pipeline.
End-to-end neural models replaced this pipeline with a single
sequence-to-sequence mapping trained with connectionist temporal
classification, attention encoder--decoder networks, and
RNN-transducers~\cite{graves2006ctc,chan2016las,graves2012rnnt}, with
the Conformer~\cite{gulati2020conformer} becoming a strong hybrid of
convolution and self-attention.
Self-supervised pretraining then reduced reliance on labelled audio
by learning speech representations from unlabelled
data~\cite{baevski2020wav2vec2,hsu2021hubert}, and weakly supervised
learning at scale produced robust multilingual systems such as
Whisper~\cite{radford2023whisper}.
Most recently, SpeechLLMs pair audio encoders with autoregressive
language backbones~\cite{chu2023qwenaudio,tang2024salmonn,stepaudio22025,kimiaudio2025,qwen2025qwen3asr},
inheriting the comprehension and instruction-following ability of
large language models.

General transcription is only one part of practical ASR. A recogniser
may also face four recognition settings beyond
unconditioned transcription.
\emph{Hotword-conditioned ASR} addresses the recognition difficulty of
rare entities and domain-specific terminology---proper nouns, product
names, and professional terms that an unconditioned model frequently
mis-transcribes---by accepting a user-supplied hotword list as
contextual bias.
\emph{Target-speaker ASR} addresses multi-talker overlap: given a short
enrollment utterance of a target speaker, the model must transcribe
only that speaker's content from overlapping speech, rather than
concatenating all talkers.
\emph{Degradation-robust ASR} handles real-world capture conditions
such as far-field pickup, additive noise, reverberation, and
transmission dropout that degrade captured speech.
\emph{Whispered-speech ASR} transcribes atypical, low-energy whispered
phonation that deviates from normal speech.
These settings differ in their side information and acoustic
conditions, but they share the same desired output: the transcript of
the requested speech.

We present \textbf{AmphionASR}, built on Qwen3-ASR-1.7B, as a shared
model for these four recognition settings.
Hotword lists and target-speaker enrollment audio enter as task
conditions, while degradation and whispered-speech examples adapt the
model to atypical acoustic inputs.
This formulation separates the recognition backbone from the
information that specifies what and under which conditions to
transcribe.
The hotword path follows prior work that already combines GLCLAP
top-$K$ retrieval, prompt injection, and GRPO~\cite{lakomkin2025contextual};
our scope is integration into the shared model and evaluation across
the recognition settings studied here.

Our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A shared interface for conditioned ASR.}
        We formulate hotword-conditioned, target-speaker,
        degradation-robust, and whispered-speech ASR within one model,
        using task instructions and optional reference inputs.
  \item \textbf{An integrated large-vocabulary hotword-conditioning pipeline.}
        We use a fine-grained dual encoder to retrieve a top-$K$ subset from a
        larger vocabulary before decoding (Recall@$50$ above $94$\,\% on
        CommonVoice) and inject the selected terms into the prompt as
        contextual bias. On GigaSpeechBench, the complete pipeline reduces
        average entity error by $55$\,\% in Mandarin and $37$\,\% in English
        relative to no hotword context~\cite{tu2026gigaspeechbench}. In the
        measured single-request, steady-state setting, online retrieval adds
        median overheads of $1.37$\,ms in English and $3.25$\,ms in Mandarin
        relative to precomputed Top-$50$ prompts.
  \item \textbf{A multi-condition evaluation.}
        We evaluate the shared model on standard ASR, contextual ASR,
        target-speaker ASR, acoustic degradations, and whispered
        speech, reporting both improvements and remaining gaps across
        the evaluated conditions.
\end{itemize}
